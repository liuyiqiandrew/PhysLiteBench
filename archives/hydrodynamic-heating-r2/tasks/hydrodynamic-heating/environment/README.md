# Optical heating in a viscous charged fluid

A uniform charged fluid fills a plane slab 0<z<d, surrounded by vacuum. Its stationary positive background makes it neutral at rest. The coordinates x,y are unbounded. Use reduced units epsilon_0=mu_0=c=1. The fluid has equilibrium number density n0, carrier charge q and mass m. The single unknown plasma frequency p, with p^2=n0*q^2/m, is common to all experiments and lies in [.85,1.15].

Consider the linear transverse response about rest. The fluid is incompressible and Newtonian, with mass density m*n0, kinematic shear viscosity nu=.04, and a local momentum relaxation force per unit mass -gamma*v with gamma=.06. Its viscous stress is eta*(grad(v)+grad(v)^T), where eta=nu*m*n0. The electric force per unit mass is q*E/m, the current is J=q*n0*v, and the electromagnetic fields obey Maxwell's equations. Magnetic forces on the perturbation are second order and are omitted. There is no other polarization, dissipative channel, pressure-driven flow or material surface conductivity. These equations and material constants define the continuum model at all listed settings.

A monochromatic plane wave is incident from z<0 with electric field along y and wavevector in the x-z plane. Its angular frequency is `frequency`, incidence angle from +z is `angle`, and the time convention is exp(-i*frequency*t). No wave is incident from z>d. The usual tangential electric and magnetic fields are continuous at each face. Both stationary slab walls impose no slip on the fluid. All measurements use the periodic steady response after transients.

Distributed weak thermostats maintain the stated material constants and locally remove the internal thermal energy generated by the driven motion. Their response does not alter the linear electromagnetic or velocity fields. A calorimeter sums that removed heat in a fixed Eulerian depth window, averaged over one drive period and unit transverse area, before subsequent spatial thermal transport. The calorimeter collects heat from both the fluid and its stationary background in the selected window, including thermalization of local momentum relaxation. Background heat is subtracted. The readout is divided by the normal incident electromagnetic power per area, at leading quadratic order in incident field amplitude. `window=[a,b]` selects a*d<z<b*d; there is no division by the window thickness. The calibrated detector has unit efficiency.

Each experiment is a dictionary with `frequency` in [.7,1.5], `thickness`=d in [.3,1.2], `angle` in [-1,1] radians, and two window fractions 0<=a<b<=1 with b-a>=.15. All controls, gamma and nu are exact. Input field amplitude cancels in the normalized readout.

`data/calibration.json` contains independent records with `input`, measured `value`, and fixed Gaussian standard deviation `sigma`. The instrument uncertainty is independent of p.

Implement `Model.fit(records)`, returning self and storing p as `plasma_frequency`. `Model.predict(experiments)` returns a finite NumPy array of shape `(len(experiments),)` in input order. Run `python -m pytest -q test_public.py`.
