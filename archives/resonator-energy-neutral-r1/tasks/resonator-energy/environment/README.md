# Mechanical energy in a resonant elastic slab

A lossless one-dimensional elastic slab occupies 0<x<length and has unit cross section. Its host mass per reference length is 1 and its unknown host stiffness is common to all preparations, in [0.7,1.6]. Every point of the host carries an independent attached point-mass oscillator. Its known mass per reference length is `resonator_mass`; its spring stiffness per reference length is resonator_mass*resonance_frequency^2. Each spring connects the local resonator displacement to the host displacement at the same reference coordinate. Neighboring resonators have no direct coupling. When resonator_mass=0, both attached mass and spring are absent. The host and springs obey exact linear elasticity; all displacements are longitudinal and small. There is no damping.

The two exterior semi-infinite leads have mass per reference length 1, stiffness 2, and no resonators. The host displacement and longitudinal traction are continuous at each slab face. No additional point mass or spring is located at a face. A source in the left lead sends a right-moving monochromatic displacement wave with real amplitude `incident_amplitude` at x=0, angular frequency `frequency`, and time convention Re[u(x)*exp(-i*frequency*t)]. There is no incoming wave from the right. The external source maintains the incident wave; reflected and transmitted waves leave along the leads. The readout is made in the harmonic scattering state after outgoing preparation transients have left, with no freely oscillating initial resonator motion.

An ideal measurement reports the total mechanical energy of the slab, averaged over one drive cycle. Its measured region includes the host and every attached resonator and spring between the two slab faces. It excludes the leads and the driving apparatus. The measurement does not change the motion. All numerical inputs use consistent dimensionless mechanical units, and the energy output uses the corresponding energy unit. Each experiment is independently prepared.

Known inputs are `length` in [0.5,2], `frequency` in [0.3,2], `resonator_mass` in [0,1.2], `resonance_frequency` in [1.5,2.5], and `incident_amplitude` in [0.01,0.04]. If resonators are present, frequency/resonance_frequency is at most 0.85. All controls are exact; only host_stiffness is unknown.

Calibration records in `data/calibration.json` contain `input`, measured energy `value`, and known independent Gaussian instrument standard deviation `sigma`. Implement `Model.fit(records)`, returning self and storing the scalar `host_stiffness`. Implement `Model.predict(experiments)`, returning a finite NumPy array of shape `(len(experiments),)` in input order.
