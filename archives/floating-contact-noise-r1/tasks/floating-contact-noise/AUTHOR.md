# Floating-contact noise, revision 1

The supplied closure is the complete unitary scattering calculation for a fixed-potential contact, evaluated at the correct floating island mean potential. It enforces zero mean island current and uses every coupling and bias. Every dc calibration is therefore exact. It does not impose the island charge constraint on fluctuations. A finite island voltage responds to incident shot noise; at counting times much longer than its charge relaxation, integrated island current fluctuations vanish per unit time. Measured terminal noise is the fixed-potential covariance transformed by P = I - G[:,2] e2^T/G22. This produces charge-conserving terminal fluctuations.

The independent verifier enumerates occupied outgoing Slater states and their particle-count probabilities in each chemical-potential window. It then integrates island charge continuity. The oracle instead uses the scattering-current A matrices. Validation checks unitarity, full fixed-potential covariance agreement, positive semidefiniteness, island/terminal conservation, disconnected Bernoulli noise, and convergence of a finite-capacitance frequency response to the counted-charge limit. Temperature zero is held by a heat-only bath; the macroscopic linear Langevin and low-frequency order of limits is public. There is no Coulomb blockade or floating-temperature ambiguity.

The only fitted parameter is alpha; the allowed interval lies below pi/4, giving unique monotone dc identification. Calibration varies both bias signs and island coupling. Hidden groups measure source auto noise, drain auto noise and their cross covariance. Noise means Cov(Q)/time, without the conventional spectral factor of two. Hidden RMS error is normalized by the group's RMS reference, tolerance .04; detector calibration noise is .006 of maximum current. Public inputs do not change after freezing.

Primary physical context: [Texier and Büttiker, Effect of incoherent scattering on shot noise correlations](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0003301), and [Beenakker and Büttiker, Suppression of shot noise in metallic diffusive conductors](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.1889). The task uses a specified ideal three-terminal scatterer rather than copying an experimental device.
