# Ionic current loops, revision 3

This is a substantive revision of the ionic-current-loops family, not a new family. Revision 1's two-dimensional local-current problem passed 3/3 plain and 3/3 hinted trials. Revision 2's closed one-dimensional ring passed 1/3 actual unhinted trials, with two physical failures; a fourth attempt was an unstarted Docker setup failure and was replaced once. Both revisions, their native evidence and causal reviews remain unchanged in `archives/screened/tasks/ionic-current-loops` and `archives/ionic-current-loops-r2`. The present revision has not been evaluated. Prior outcomes do not qualify it.

The supplied model now solves the full variable-conductivity elliptic problem, including nonzero local circulating currents. It is exact for an open-circuit homogenized boundary condition that fixes both mean charge currents to zero while allowing a constant harmonic electric field. The physical isolated periodic cell instead has zero electromotive force around either cycle. That condition fixes the harmonic electric field, not the mean current. The source retains local charge conservation, curl-free fields and positive dissipation; those checks cannot select the correct global boundary ensemble.

In units with the common diffusivity set to one, define

    g = sum z_i*d_i*c_i = 3*c1,
    sigma = sum d_i*c_i = 2*c0+5*c1,
    J = -grad(g)+sigma*E,
    E = E0-grad(phi),
    A = -div(sigma*grad).

The physical potential is periodic and solves `A phi = lap(g)`, with E0=0. The source first solves that same equation. For each coordinate a it then solves `A psi_a=-partial_a sigma`. The corresponding unit imposed-field response is `F_a=e_a-grad(psi_a)`. Its averaged charge current defines a symmetric positive conductance matrix `G_ja=<sigma F_a,j>`. The source chooses `E0=-G^-1<J_base>` and adds `sum E0_a F_a` to the electric field. This correctly solves its alternate boundary-value problem; the matrix solve, signs and local field response are not implementation defects.

All species use the same physical electric field. Their fluxes are `j_i=-d_i grad(c_i)+z_i d_i c_i E`, and initial concentration rates are `-div(j_i)`. The unknown common D multiplies all fluxes and concentration rates, but cancels from the field equation. In both ensembles each integrated species amount is conserved and `sum z_i dot(c_i)=0`. For the ideal-mixture free energy, its rate is minus `sum integral |j_i|^2/(d_i c_i)` plus `integral E.J`. That last integral vanishes for the physical periodic potential, and also for the source because its mean current is zero. Thus both closures dissipate the same free energy while differing physically in the loop condition.

Every calibration preparation has phase zero, so both cation profiles are invariant under simultaneous inversion of x and y. Potentials are even, fields and currents are odd, and both harmonic fields and mean currents vanish. Local currents are nevertheless nonzero: their smallest RMS in the calibration settings is .05519. Four amplitude settings, two species and four ordinary Fourier readouts give 32 distinct settings, repeated nine times independently. Calibration therefore tests the complete local circulating-field response and uniquely identifies D. The old pointwise-zero-current approximation cannot pass this calibration by rescaling D: its optimal noiseless calibration RMS is .001610 against fixed instrument sigma .001, giving expected reduced chi-square about 3.59. The new source and physical oracle agree on calibration to 2.23e-16.

All profiles and detector definitions are public. The hidden diagnostic uses the fixed ordinary species-0 Fourier coefficient with wavevector (1,0) and phase pi/4; preparation phase and amplitudes vary. No adaptive or cancellation-tuned detector is used. The prototype first screened a different three-wave profile, `c0=1+.4 cos x+.4 cos y`, `c1=1+.75 cos(x+y+phase)`. That profile gave too little robust separation for ordinary fundamental readouts. The complete original screen and the subsequent amplitude screen are preserved in `prototypes/`. The final preparation contains x, y and x+y components, and the shared x component has a relative phase that translation cannot remove. The positive lower concentration bound is .05. The calibration has genuine two-dimensional currents, so this is not a disguised repetition of the one-dimensional pointwise-zero-current task.

The oracle uses a Fourier potential solve with a constant-conductivity Laplacian preconditioner and zero-mode gauge. The independent private reference discretizes conservative currents on faces of a periodic square finite-volume grid, solves a separately assembled sparse scalar-potential matrix, and projects concentration rates by discrete integration by parts. It uses 64/128 grids with Richardson extrapolation. It has no harmonic-field corrector or zero-current constraint. Independent 96/192 refinement and Fourier 63-to-95 checks quantify its error. A separate prototype finite-volume solution of the source's alternate boundary ensemble agrees with the completed source to 1.52e-8.

The final report is `results/ionic-current-loops-r3-validation.json`. Physical oracle/reference hidden disagreement is at most 6.61e-8; finite-volume refinement changes predictions by at most 5.30e-8, and Fourier refinement by 3.06e-16. Public corner checks are within 2.09e-8. Local charge balance is within 5.48e-12 and free-energy dissipation within 6.14e-12. Reflection reverses the mean current to roundoff. Noiseless fitting recovers every one of 41 diffusivities across [.5,2] to 4.45e-16.

The true D is 1.13. Calibration and validation-noise seeds are 119151 and 119157. Both data copies are identical, and sigma=.001 is a fixed instrument value independent of the parameter and signal. The actual fit gives D=1.1299925998 and reduced chi-square .98807. All 256 noisy calibrations pass, every physical oracle passes, and every completed shortcut fails all three diagnostic groups while passing symmetric anchors. The largest noisy oracle error is .000414; the smallest shortcut group error is .10714. Nominal shortcut group errors are .11789, .10745 and .11654. The previous .04 normalized RMS grading threshold, 3% parameter threshold and chi-square 1.5 threshold are unchanged. Local controls give oracle 9/9 in 1.08 seconds and shortcut 6 passes plus 3 intended hidden failures in 1.79 seconds.

The public starter leaves only fitting unfinished and contains no unused repair imports or hidden corrective branch. The exact neutral instruction permits replacing any code while preserving the documented API. A failed trial must be reviewed causally: retaining the zero-mean-current ensemble is a physical failure; deriving the zero-EMF condition but implementing it incorrectly is a mathematical or coding failure. Fixed-fitted-D diagnostics should make that distinction concrete.

Primary context is [Warren, Non-Faradaic electric currents in the Nernst–Planck equations and action-at-a-distance diffusiophoresis in crossed salt gradients, PRL 124, 248004 (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06016). It establishes that local electroneutrality permits circulating ionic currents in crossed salt gradients. The present torus harmonic-sector comparison follows separately from charge continuity and the specified loop electromotive forces; it is not attributed to that paper's boundary geometry.
