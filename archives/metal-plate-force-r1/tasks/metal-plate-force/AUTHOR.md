# Metal plate force, revision 1

The specified apparatus is an equilibrium local Drude model with strictly positive residual damping. It is not a model-selection question about experimental metals: the permittivity, magnetic permeability and fluctuation-dissipation assumption are given. The calibration is ground-state pressure, while predictions also include finite-temperature pressure.

For imaginary frequency xi and transverse wave number k, define q=sqrt(k²+xi²) and q_m=sqrt(k²+epsilon(i*xi)*xi²). Maxwell matching gives r_TE=(q-q_m)/(q+q_m) and r_TM=(epsilon*q-q_m)/(epsilon*q+q_m). At fixed positive damping, xi²*epsilon(i*xi) tends to zero as xi tends to zero. Thus the normal metal has r_TE=0 and r_TM=1 at the static endpoint. A static magnetic field penetrates this nonmagnetic material.

The completed shortcut uses the exact dissipative reflection coefficients at every positive imaginary frequency. At zero frequency alone, it uses the screened transverse response of a lossless plasma, r_TE=(k-sqrt(k²+omega_p²))/(k+sqrt(k²+omega_p²)). Its mathematical integrations, prime weight, pressure normalization and fitting objective are complete. The physical approximation extends optical screening to a static magnetic field.

In ground-state pressure the imaginary frequency is integrated continuously, so changing one endpoint has zero measure. Both models coincide on every calibration input for every allowed plasma frequency. At positive temperature, the discrete zero-frequency fluctuation has half weight in the Matsubara sum and contributes finite pressure. The missing distinction therefore survives an accurate calibration fit.

The oracle computes the electromagnetic momentum-flux pressure. The independent reference computes the interaction free energy using surface impedances and differentiates it with respect to separation at fixed temperature and material parameters. Its static reflection is evaluated from the positive-frequency impedance at 1e-15, rather than assigned the oracle's zero-mode value. Adaptive static quadrature, different spectral quadrature orders and a five-point separation derivative provide numerical checks. Additional validation covers the static limit, the classical Drude pressure -T*zeta(3)/(8*pi*a³), the ideal-reflector ground-state pressure -pi²/(240*a⁴), and convergence to zero temperature at fixed positive damping.

Calibration contains 192 observations: 16 independent repeats at 12 separation/damping settings. Its standard deviation is 2e-6/a⁴, fixed by the known separation and independent of the plasma frequency or response. Both controls fit omega_p=6.499931855 with reduced chi-square 0.96436. All 256 independent noise realizations pass calibration, parameter recovery and oracle predictions. The largest oracle hidden error is 0.00002025 and the smallest shortcut group error is 0.15839, against a group RMS relative-pressure threshold of 0.04. The actual shortcut errors are 0.386, 0.158 and 0.428. The parameter is identifiable; the full calibration loss has one minimum on a 101-point scan over its stated range.

The maximum pressure versus free-energy derivative discrepancy, scaled by a⁴, is 1.70e-10. The largest relative discrepancy at parameter and apparatus corners is 1.53e-8. Actual isolated verification gives seven oracle passes and four shortcut passes with exactly three hidden prediction failures. Calibration seed 948191 and noise seed 948193 are fixed. Scientific and local-control evidence is in [the validation report](../../results/metal-plate-force-validation.json); independent peer review is in [the Brownian review](../../results/brownian-cross-review-metal-plate-force.json).

The normal-metal static mode distinction is discussed in Hoye, Brevik, Aarseth and Milton, [“Does the Transverse Electric Zero Mode Contribute to the Casimir Effect for a Metal?”](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0212125). The task uses the explicitly specified dissipative material response, without asking the agent to choose among competing descriptions of real materials.
