# Thermal instability of a two-species gravitating gas

A classical gas is confined inside a rigid spherical reflecting wall of
radius R. It contains light particles of mass m and heavy particles of
mass chi*m. The total gas mass is M. A fraction q of that total mass is in
heavy particles: M_h=q*M and M_l=(1-q)*M. Thus q is a mass fraction, not a
particle-number fraction. The numbers of both species are separately
fixed, and neither species converts into the other. Both species can
move throughout the vessel.

Every pair interacts through Newtonian gravity with one unknown strength
G in [0.7,1.4] mm^3/(mass-unit second^2). The same G applies to every run.
There is no central body, rotation, external potential, or surface mass.
The wall supports pressure and does not move. A non-gravitating ideal
thermal reservoir imposes a common temperature T on both species. It
exchanges heat but supplies no force, particles, or gas volume. Coupling
energy is negligible.

Use the smooth, collisional Boltzmann mean-field limit. Both species have
local Maxwell velocity distributions at the same T. Define the reported
specific temperature theta=k_B*T/m in mm^2/second^2, always using the LIGHT
particle mass m. Each stationary state is spherical, has regular finite
central densities, and is in hydrostatic equilibrium in the gravitational
potential generated by both species. The total enclosed masses at the
wall are M_l and M_h.

The readout is theta at the first loss of local thermodynamic stability
as the imposed temperature is lowered from the dilute stable branch.
Stability means a local minimum of the gas Helmholtz free energy under
infinitesimal smooth spherical perturbations, with T, R, and each species'
total particle number fixed. Follow the regular equilibrium branch from
dilute states to its first loss. This is a mean-field local threshold, not
a collapse waiting time or a global free-energy minimum. Finite-particle
activation, binary formation, short-distance encounters, and collisionless
dynamical stability are outside this description.

An experiment is a dictionary with `mass` M in [0.8,1.6] mass-units,
`radius` R in [0.7,1.5] mm, `mass_ratio` chi in [1,5], and
`heavy_fraction` q in [0,0.5]. At q=0 the heavy species is absent. At chi=1
the two species have equal masses. Predictions must cover the full domain.

`data/calibration.json` is a list of independent records. Each contains
an `input` experiment, a noisy `value`, and its known additive Gaussian
standard deviation `sigma`, in output units. Calibration uses preparations
with q=0 or chi=1.

Implement `Model.fit(records)`, returning self and setting `self.gravity`
to the fitted G. Implement `Model.predict(experiments)`, returning a finite
one-dimensional NumPy array with one critical theta per experiment, in
input order. Prediction inputs contain no measured value or sigma.
NumPy and SciPy are available. The supplied response is a starting model
and may be changed; preserve these methods and the parameter attribute.
