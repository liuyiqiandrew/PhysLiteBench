# Two probes coupled to an oscillator: concept review

Verdict: the proposed physics is correct, but reject this as a distinct new expansion candidate. It repeats the central mechanism, calibration loophole, and reference of archived `qubit-control-neutral-r2`, whose manifest records 3/3 passes. Reducing four modes to one and fixing preparation/readout rotations simplifies that apparatus without introducing a new missing physical effect. No implementation or evaluation was performed for this review.

## Derivation and conventions

Use `hbar=k_B=1`, `S=c1*sigma_z1+c2*sigma_z2`, `theta=omega*t`, and a bare thermal oscillator initially independent of `|+x,+x>`. Define

```
G = (g/omega)^2*(1-cos(theta))*coth(omega/(2*T)),
Phi = (g/omega)^2*(theta-sin(theta)).
```

At zero temperature, the coth factor is one. For eigenvalues `s,s'` of `S`, the reduced coherence multiplier is

```
exp[-G*(s-s')^2] * exp[i*Phi*(s^2-s'^2)].
```

This follows directly by completing the oscillator square or by the terminating Magnus expansion. The latter's second term is proportional to `S^2`, since the oscillator force commutator is a scalar. For the proposed readout,

```
eta = 4*c1*c2*Phi
P++ = 1/4 + cos(eta)*(exp(-4*G*c1^2)+exp(-4*G*c2^2))/4
      + (exp(-4*G*(c1+c2)^2)+exp(-4*G*(c1-c2)^2))/8.
```

A stationary classical Gaussian force with covariance
`g^2*coth(omega/(2*T))*cos(omega*(t-s))` gives the same `G` and replaces `cos(eta)` by one. It includes the full shared classical covariance; the discrepancy is the coherent interaction generated by the quantum commutator. With `c2=0`, `S^2=c1^2*I` and the quantum phase is global. Both descriptions give `P++=(1+exp(-4*G*c1^2))/2`. Calibration identifies positive `g` if it contains nonzero coupling and times away from exact oscillator revivals. It determines only `g^2` if the sign of `g` is allowed to vary.

The stated complete Hamiltonian and exclusion of a counterterm remove the ambiguity. Completing its square includes the energy shift `-g^2*S^2/omega`; this must not be silently canceled. Adding `+g^2*S^2/omega` would change `Phi` to `-(g/omega)^2*sin(theta)`, rather than leave the proposed law intact. Specify that coupling is off during bare thermal preparation and applied for the measurement interval, with no separate switching spin operation. There is no need for a weak-coupling or Markov approximation.

## Reference feasibility

A finite Fock reference is straightforward. Construct `H_s=omega*N+g*s*(a+a†)` for each of the four spin eigenvalues, exponentiate these matrices, and form `A=sum_s U_s/4`. Then `P++=Tr(A*rho_thermal*A†)`. This independently evolves the stated Hamiltonian without using the displacement phase formula. Refine the Fock cutoff against both thermal occupation and the largest transient conditional displacement, which can reach `2*abs(g*s/omega)`. The archived task already demonstrates this route and its cutoff checks.

## Archive overlap

The [archived public apparatus](../archives/qubit-control-neutral-r2/task/environment/README.md) has two probes coupled longitudinally to four thermal oscillators, with individual-probe calibration and general preparation/readout rotations. Its [AUTHOR.md](../archives/qubit-control-neutral-r2/task/AUTHOR.md) gives precisely the two coherence factors above, with mode sums, and identifies omission of the commutator phase as the shortcut. Its [manifest](../archives/qubit-control-neutral-r2/manifest.json) records that this quantum-bath revision passed all three Luna High trials before subsequent revisions added other physics.

Superconducting heat is a less direct overlap: it concerns energy-resolved displacement coherence and heat transfer, not this finite-time induced spin interaction. That distinction does not remedy the exact qubit-control precedent. The proposed mechanism is also discussed in primary work on [nonclassical pure dephasing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-11502-4), but literature soundness does not make it a new benchmark family. Do not package or rerun this simplification as a fresh candidate for the requested third addition.
