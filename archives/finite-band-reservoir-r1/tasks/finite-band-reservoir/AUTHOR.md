# Finite-band reservoir, revision 1

The supplied model computes the exact single-particle spectrum of the contacted orbital and chain, including its continuum and all isolated poles with their correct positive residues. It fills every mode with the reservoir Fermi function. This is a complete coupled-Gibbs approximation. The public preparation is instead a product state followed by a sudden contact quench and isolated quadratic evolution. Spatially localized modes of that Hamiltonian need not equilibrate with its continuum.

The apparatus states the initially occupied orbital, grand canonical uncoupled chain at chemical potential zero, instantaneous contact, subsequent unitary evolution, and thermodynamic-limit-before-time-average order. No extra relaxation channel is present. The time average removes coherent oscillations between distinct localized modes; it does not assign new occupations to those modes. The public description gives no spectral classification or correction formula.

With chain hopping one, the endpoint Green function is

    g_s(E) = [E-sqrt(E²-4)]/2,

with the retarded analytic branch. The contacted orbital Green function is G(E)=[E-epsilon-V² g_s(E)]^-1. The continuum density is -Im G/pi. Outside the band, each real root E_b of the denominator has dot weight Z_b=[1-V² g_s'(E_b)]^-1. The source includes all of these quantities exactly, and its occupation is

    integral_band A(E) f(E)dE + sum_b Z_b f(E_b).

For the prepared product state, a localized eigenmode has initial occupation

    n_b = Z_b*[1 + V²*integral_band rho_s(E) f(E)/(E_b-E)² dE],

where rho_s(E)=sqrt(4-E²)/(2*pi). Its contribution to the dot readout is Z_b*n_b. The physical oracle uses these occupations and the same continuum term as the source. These are populations of normal modes of the full Hamiltonian; changing a Fermi function or dropping a pole would not reproduce the quench preparation.

The independent verifier diagonalizes a finite real tridiagonal orbital-plus-chain Hamiltonian. A discrete sine transform projects the initial uncoupled bath thermal covariance into its normal-mode basis. It takes the diagonal ensemble of this initial covariance and reconstructs dot occupation. It contains no surface Green function, isolated-pole solver or continuum-population formula. Values for chain lengths128,256,512 remove the first two inverse-length corrections. Checks at256,512,1024 establish convergence independently. This finite-chain diagonal-average route is a computational reference for the same limiting local diagonal ensemble; it is not a change to the public order of limits. For the infinite chain, integrable continuum amplitudes from the initially local orbital disperse, and off-diagonal continuum energies dephase locally. The thermal incoming continuum retains its Fermi occupation. Bound-continuum terms and terms between distinct isolated poles vanish under the specified time average. Because the poles remain separated from the band, their eigenfunctions are exponentially localized; their finite-chain energies and initial-covariance projections converge exponentially with chain length. The residual continuum correction in a finite-chain diagonal ensemble vanishes with inverse chain length: the initial orbital contribution contains sums of fourth powers of delocalized amplitudes, and the local quench changes each extended-mode population by a vanishing amount. These separate statements establish the shared local limiting value here; no generic interchange of infinite-size and infinite-time limits is assumed.
