# Separatrix crossing: bounded feasibility

Verdict: the mechanism is physically coherent and merits a bounded dynamical prototype. It is not ready for a task package. No Hamiltonian-trajectory convergence, calibration noise study, grader or model evaluation has been run.

Use a classical isolated particle of unit mass with the controlled Hamiltonian

    H = p^2/2 + q^4/4 - s*q^2/2 + delta*s^(3/2)*q.

The positive scale s increases once along a known smooth ramp from .05 to a chosen endpoint. The asymmetry delta is known, for example .15–.25. The particle is prepared on a finite annulus: initial action J is uniform on [J0,1.2*J0], and the canonical angle is independently uniform. J0 is the one unknown preparation scale, tentatively [.12,.16]. There is no bath, friction or noise during the ramp. Average final energy over the preparation ensemble at each finite ramp duration, then take duration to infinity. Energy is read after the parameter is held fixed. No final orbital-phase average is needed for this energy reading.

The initial angle must mean canonical orbital phase, not polar angle in the physical q,p plane. A finite-width ensemble is essential to the proposed operational definition; this assessment does not claim capture probabilities for a single initial phase point, or interchange a zero-width limit with the slow-ramp limit. The model is classical throughout; taking a quantum adiabatic limit instead can change the result.

The completed source would accurately calculate the full enclosed phase volume Phi(E,s) and conserve it throughout the ramp. This is the usual ergodic adiabatic closure extended across disconnected energy shells. Its mathematics is meaningful. Its physical assumption fails after capture because a trajectory remains in one lobe rather than exploring both disconnected wells. It is stronger than blindly returning the initial state after a cycle, and uses every preparation and ramp control.

For fixed delta, q=sqrt(s)*x and p=s*P show that both separatrix lobe areas scale as s^(3/2). Their positive growth rates have the same ratio as their areas. At capture, the classical area-growth rule gives probabilities P_i=A_i/(A_left+A_right), and the outgoing lobe action is P_i times the incoming action. Each captured branch then preserves its own action. This is an analytically simple crossing geometry; it has no saddle birth along the positive-s path and no repeated crossing if the lobes keep growing.

The bounded static check in area_check.py computes the saddle, exact one-dimensional phase areas and periods, and final branch energies. Nine points cover delta=.15,.20,.25 and incoming J=.12,.16,.20 at final s=2. Mean total energy lies between -1.601 and -1.052 in the Hamiltonian's stated zero, and the global-volume source differs by 9.78–17.90%. No background has been added or subtracted to create this difference. Mean kinetic energy is also positive and differs by 27.2–64.4%; it is an optional readout, not needed for the simpler same-energy calibration and test. These numbers are static candidate predictions, not an independent validation of capture dynamics or a full-domain margin proof.

Noncrossing calibration can use the same asymmetric potential and ramps ending at s=.10–.20. Throughout the sampled geometry, the largest separatrix action there is below .12, so the entire proposed initial band stays on connected outer orbits. Both source and physical adiabatic evolution then exactly preserve the same action. The final energy is strictly increasing with initial action because dE/dJ is the positive orbital frequency. Writing J=J0*z with fixed uniform z in [1,1.2] proves that the ensemble mean calibration energy increases strictly with J0. This supplies a real identifiable preparation parameter without assuming unknown mass remains visible in a strict adiabatic limit. The endpoint bounds must be checked over the entire declared domain in a later prototype.

A suitable independent reference directly integrates Hamilton's equations for deterministic quadrature points in the initial action/angle annulus through several increasing ramp durations. It must independently construct the initial orbit-time distribution, refine action and angle grids, refine the integrator step/tolerance, and separate these errors from the slow-ramp limit. Compare the final mean energy and lobe fractions to the static capture construction. No capture rule belongs inside that reference. Monitor energy-work balance, each final lobe action, noncrossing calibration, symmetric delta=0 equivalence and changing ramp shape. A single slow trajectory run or one phase grid is insufficient: crossing basins become finely interleaved and the error need not be monotone in sweep duration. No reference runtime or convergence claim is made here.

Reject the symmetric-only version as a discriminator: equal lobes can make the globally mixed and captured energy predictions coincide. Reject an unspecified 'slow double-well sweep' without exact ensemble and limit order. Reject an unexplained equiprobable split, use of final lobe volumes instead of growth at crossing for general paths, or treating numerical separatrix resolution failures as physical benchmark errors. For this homothetic path the growth fractions simplify, but that fact must not be generalized to arbitrary ramps.

The archive search found no separatrix/Neishtadt entry in existing AUTHOR or ASSESSMENT files. The closest actual source read is archived collisionless-trap: it distinguishes conserved noncrossing actions from a single thermal state. This proposal has related adiabatic/ergodicity physics but introduces a true change in phase-space connectivity. It should disclose that relationship.

Primary sources read:

- [Buerkle et al., arXiv:1903.04834](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.04834), phase-space discussion and Methods. It supports finite-width preparation and persistent classical crossing effects in slow sweeps. Its cyclic condensate example is not the proposed quartic one-way apparatus.
- [Stabel and Anglin, New Journal of Physics 24, 113052](https://inspirehep.net/files/6605eec5cbece001798c0edfc5e0c46d), sections 2 and 2.1. The classical area-growth capture rule and quartic double-well example support the proposed construction; quantum and classical limiting procedures differ.
- [Neishtadt, arXiv:1705.04347](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.04347), introduction and sections 2.1–2.2. The nondegenerate saddle, positive area growth and ensemble interpretation require checking before applying the probabilistic averaging rule.
