# Thermal relaxation of a modulated two-level system

Use hbar=kB=1. A two-level system with fixed basis |e>,|g> has Hamiltonian H(t)=[1+A cos(Omega t)] sigma_z/2, with sigma_z|e>=|e> and sigma_z|g>=-|g>. The drive is a prescribed classical source. The observable is the probability of |e>, regardless of the sign of the instantaneous splitting. Drive phase at preparation is zero.

A stationary bosonic reservoir at temperature T=0.2 couples weakly through sigma_x tensor B. Its unsymmetrized two-sided correlation spectrum is G(omega)=integral dt exp(i omega t) <B(t)B(0)> = coupling * omega exp(-|omega|/1.5)/(1-exp(-omega/T)). At omega=0 use its continuous value coupling*T. Positive omega corresponds to energy deposited into the reservoir by the undriven system. There are no other relaxation or dephasing channels, no backaction on the drive, and no change of bath temperature.

The shared unknown coupling is in [0.002,0.008]. Predict the leading weak-reservoir-coupling dynamics: take the reservoir coupling to zero at fixed nonzero Omega, fixed A and fixed bath spectrum shape, observing relaxation-scale times. All dissipative rates are much smaller than Omega, and observation times are long compared with the bath correlation time. Do not assume that the drive is slow compared with the level splitting or the bath correlation time. Corrections beyond this controlled weak-coupling limit are outside the requested model. Reservoir energy shifts affect only phases and do not affect the measured population.

Every input includes amplitude A in [0,2.5], frequency Omega in [0.6,2], initial_population in [0,1] and cycles, an integer from 1 to 200. Prepare a diagonal density matrix with the stated |e> probability at t=0 and measure after cycles complete drive periods, t=2*pi*cycles/Omega. No coherences are initially prepared. The task uses the names `amplitude`, `frequency`, `initial_population`, and `cycles` in input dictionaries.

Calibration has amplitude zero and varies elapsed time and initial population. Independent Gaussian measurement errors have the recorded sigma. Fit one coupling shared by all measurements. Implement Model.fit(records), returning self, and Model.predict(experiments), returning a finite NumPy array with one probability per experiment. Store the estimate as Model.coupling. Data records contain input, value and sigma fields.
