# Angular transport in a two-branch phonon gas

A homogeneous three-dimensional insulating crystal has two isotropic acoustic phonon branches with linear dispersion omega_i(q)=v_i*|q|. Their known speeds are v0=1 and v1=0.6 mm/s. There are three degenerate copies of branch 0 and one of branch 1. Treat both spectra as unbounded linear Debye spectra in the low-temperature regime. The branch heat capacities in common reduced units are C0=3/v0^3 and C1=1/v1^3. They are fixed during each small perturbation. Spatial variation is along x; propagation directions cover the full sphere with mu the cosine relative to +x. Preparations are axisymmetric and the crystal is infinite.

Work in linear response about uniform Bose equilibrium at temperature T0=1 in reduced temperature units and zero drift. Phonon number is not conserved. The two independent collision mechanisms use local relaxation models. Resistive scattering randomizes propagation directions within each branch, exchanging crystal momentum with the stationary lattice while conserving that branch's energy. Its common unknown rate nu_R lies in [0.15,0.6] s^-1. Normal collisions can exchange energy and crystal momentum between branches. They conserve total phonon energy and total crystal momentum, and have one common known rate normal_rate in [0,3] s^-1 for both branches. Each collision class relaxes toward the local maximum-entropy Bose distribution subject to precisely the quantities it conserves. Linearize these local target distributions about the stated equilibrium. Relaxation rates are independent of phonon frequency and direction. There are no other collisions, boundaries, heat sources or externally maintained branch populations.

Define f_i(x,mu,t) as the frequency-integrated angular energy perturbation in branch i divided by its equilibrium angular heat capacity. Its angular average is therefore a branch temperature departure. Write angular averages as <f>=(1/2) integral[-1,1] f dmu. Total energy departure is sum_i C_i <f_i>; the heat flux is sum_i C_i v_i <mu*f_i>. Each phonon carries crystal momentum hbar*q and energy hbar*v_i*|q|. Free streaming in branch i is at velocity v_i*mu along x. All equations refer to the ideal linear response defined here; no closure in angular moments is imposed.

Each input contains a 2x3 array initial, wavenumber in [0,3] mm^-1, normal_rate, time in [0,8] s, branch (0,1 or -1), moment (0,1 or 2), and quadrature (cosine or sine). Initially

f_i(x,mu,0) = [initial[i][0]*P0(mu)+initial[i][1]*P1(mu)+initial[i][2]*P2(mu)] cos(wavenumber*x),
P0=1, P1=mu, P2=(3*mu^2-1)/2.

The sum of the absolute values of all six initial coefficients is at most 0.2. The requested observable is the specified spatial cosine/sine coefficient of M_i,l=<P_l f_i> for branch 0 or1. Branch -1 instead reports the heat-capacity-weighted mean sum_i C_i M_i,l / sum_i C_i. For nonzero wavenumber, a Fourier coefficient is twice the spatial average against the corresponding cosine or sine over one period. At zero wavenumber the cosine coefficient is the uniform value and the sine coefficient is zero. Outputs are reduced temperature departures, not fluxes with a hidden velocity factor.

Calibration observes homogeneous P2 anisotropy decay in both branches with several nonzero normal rates. It identifies the one common resistive rate. Settings are exact and measurement errors are independent Gaussian with the supplied sigma. Implement Model.fit(records), returning self and setting resistive_rate, and Model.predict(experiments), returning a finite NumPy array of shape `(len(experiments),)`. Read input/value/sigma records from data/calibration.json.
