For the rigid Hamiltonian, equilibrium is canonical in angles and their conjugate momenta. Write the kinetic energy as one half theta_dot^T G(theta) theta_dot, then integrate exp[-p^T G(theta)^{-1} p/2] over the canonical momenta. The resulting angular density has the factor sqrt(det G(theta)) multiplying exp(-U). When the first joint is mechanically clamped before equilibration, the remaining one-dimensional kinetic coefficient is constant, which explains why ordinary angular Boltzmann weighting fits calibration.
