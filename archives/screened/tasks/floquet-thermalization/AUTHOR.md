# Floquet bath calorimetry, revision 2

Revision 1 tested instantaneous thermal rates against Floquet population dynamics. Its complete source, controls, validation and run provenance are preserved in `archives/floquet-thermalization-r1`. Revision 2 supplies correct Floquet population rates and instead tests the energy measured by two reservoir calorimeters. No revision-1 measurement is reclassified.

For H(t)=[1+A cos(Omega t)] sigma_z/2, the transition phase has harmonics at epsilon_n=1+n Omega with weights w_n=J_n(A/Omega)^2. A downward transition in channel n deposits epsilon_n into its reservoir; an upward transition deposits -epsilon_n. Signed frequencies are needed even when a channel exchanges energy against the fixed state labels. The two reservoir spectra obey G_b(-epsilon)=exp(-epsilon/T_b) G_b(epsilon).

At unit common coupling, define d_b=sum_n w_n G_b(epsilon_n), u_b=sum_n w_n G_b(-epsilon_n), h_db=sum_n w_n epsilon_n G_b(epsilon_n), and h_ub=-sum_n w_n epsilon_n G_b(-epsilon_n). The population relaxes with rate gamma sum_b(d_b+u_b) toward sum_b u_b/sum_b(d_b+u_b). Averaging that exact exponential over the requested interval gives pbar. The calorimeter reads gamma [h_db pbar+h_ub(1-pbar)]. Positive heat is reservoir energy gain per unit time over the entire interval. The observable is not the final instantaneous current.

The completed shortcut uses exactly those population rates, including every bath, amplitude and frequency. It assigns energy +1 or -1 to each jump when evaluating heat, so h_db=d_b and h_ub=-u_b. This is a complete quasienergy bookkeeping closure, not a rate solver error. It identifies the system's fixed quasienergy change with the reservoir energy and loses the drive's channel-dependent work. All no-drive population and calorimetry records are exactly shared. Two temperatures ensure that even undriven stationary individual bath currents are nonzero. Driven population predictions also remain identical between the controls; a separate hidden population check confirms this.

The public specification fixes the microscopic sigma_x couplings, spectra, drive phase, preparation, bath-energy measurement and fixed-drive weak-coupling order. Interaction/preparation energy beyond that order is excluded. The apparatus does not prescribe a quasienergy calorimeter. At A=0, total bath heat equals the decrease of system energy; under driving the bath channels can receive drive work. We do not assert that a change of quasienergy alone equals the physical system energy at arbitrary drive phase. Population-only secular dynamics omit small within-cycle corrections needed to infer drive work directly; bath calorimetry is instead obtained from energy-resolved jump counting.

The oracle sums Bessel weights and integrates the population analytically. The independent verifier Fourier transforms the exact accumulated transition phase, builds a bath-energy tilted two-state population generator, propagates its characteristic function, and differentiates it numerically. This checks both channel energy signs and finite-interval population weighting. A further validator check integrates the time-domain bath energy kernel i C'_b(tau) against the directly phase-averaged transition correlation. It uses the exponential-cutoff Bose correlation series and no Bessel energy-weighted sum. Fine/coarse time-domain integrals agree with oracle energy coefficients to 3.94e-8 and refine by at most 1.75e-7. The tilted verifier agrees to 5.86e-15, including independent counting-step and Fourier-resolution checks.

Calibration has 144 records at zero amplitude, varied initial populations and durations, and both calorimeters. Independent Gaussian standard deviations are .002 for population and 2e-6 for heat. The shared scalar coupling is identifiable from both relaxation and heat scales. Generation seed is 14121; the 256 additional noise realizations use 24121. Ordinary validation reads the checked-in data; only `--generate` changes both public/private copies.

The hidden heat metric is group RMSE divided by the group's reference RMS (floor1e-6), with limit .04. Current-data oracle errors are at most 5.05e-5; shortcut errors are .716–1.623. All 256 noisy calibration fits pass; the largest coupling error is .109%. At the fitted-parameter extrema the oracle heat error is at most .00122 while the smallest shortcut error is .7158. This is a calibration-noise study plus hidden sensitivity at its parameter extrema, not 256 independent hidden experiments. The extra population check uses absolute error .005.

Local isolated public/private controls: oracle8 passed; shortcut5 passed and only the3 intended heat groups failed. Independent checks also cover static energy balance, sideband normalization, parameter/input corners and nonnegative stationary reservoir entropy production. Scientific reports are in `results/floquet-thermalization-r2-validation.json`; agent difficulty for revision2 is pending.

Primary context: [Szczygielski, Gelbwaser-Klimovsky and Alicki, Markovian master equation and thermodynamics of a two-level system in a strong laser field](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5665). The task uses its own specified commuting sinusoidal Hamiltonian and reservoir spectra.
