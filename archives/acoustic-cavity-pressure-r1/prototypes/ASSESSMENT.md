# Closed-cavity mean pressure: prototype

This is a new proposal, not a packaged task or an evaluation result. An archive-name and author-text scan found no prior fixed-mass acoustic-fluid mean-pressure task. Related archived surface-wave and elastic-resonator tasks concern elastic boundary conditions and stored energy; the electric-dipole force task concerns optical momentum transfer. This proposal instead tests second-order mean-state redistribution in a closed fluid.

The apparatus is a one-dimensional, isothermal barotropic Newtonian liquid between fixed rigid walls. Its total mass is rho0*L, and p(rho)=p0+B*((rho/rho0)^5-1)/5 is the constitutive equation at the maintained temperature. rho0=B=1 in the chosen units. The constant positive longitudinal viscosity is the only unknown. An actuator imposes the Eulerian force per volume epsilon*f*sin(m*pi*x/L)*cos(omega*t). The thermostat removes dissipated heat without exerting momentum. No mass crosses either wall. The system reaches a periodic steady state before taking epsilon to zero. These conditions close both mean pressure and thermal history; no universal radiation-pressure law is assumed.

Calibration reads the first-harmonic endwall-pressure amplitude divided by epsilon. Both controls solve the complete linear viscous acoustic field exactly. The proposed hidden readout is mean normal wall traction minus rest pressure, divided by epsilon squared. The completed shortcut computes the nonlinear equation-of-state rectification from its linear density while leaving the Eulerian mean density unchanged. It has nonzero wall signals, preserves total mass and equal endwall forces, and fits calibration exactly. It is a local mean-state approximation, not an unfinished pressure formula.

For a single standing sine mode, the first-harmonic density and velocity are in temporal quadrature. Mean continuity therefore forces the second-order mean velocity to vanish. Mean momentum requires p2+rho0*mean(v1^2) to be spatially constant. The mean viscous traction is zero. The fixed total mass then determines that constant:

    C = rho0*mean_x,t(v1^2) + 0.5*p''(rho0)*mean_x,t(rho1^2).

The endwall force per area is C. The source instead uses 0.5*p''*mean_t(rho1_wall^2). At resonance and EOS exponent5 its prediction is 4/3 of the correct value. The distinction is absent from all first-harmonic calibration; a new fit cannot absorb it without spoiling those measurements.

The prototype's independent calculation solves first-harmonic fields on a staggered finite-volume grid, then solves discrete mean continuity, local momentum balance and the fixed-mass condition for the mean density and velocity. It does not use the oracle wall-pressure expression. Across72 combinations of length, mode, detuning, drive and viscosity, 128/256 Richardson results agree within2.35e-6 relative. Source errors range20.39–46.92%; every signal is nonzero. Mass and momentum residuals are below2e-14, and first-order drive work equals viscous dissipation within1.87e-13. Calibration amplitudes are strictly decreasing throughout the viscosity interval; the sampled minimum negative slope is .348.

The physical concept was independently checked by physics_review. Full staging remains subject to root approval; it would need fixed-noise calibration,256 noisy fits, independent-reference refinement, completed controls and final source review. Retaining unchanged mean density is the intended physical failure. Recognizing redistribution but making a perturbation, factor or discretization mistake must be classified separately.

Primary background: F. E. Borgnis, [Acoustic Radiation Pressure of Plane Compressional Waves](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.25.653), Rev. Mod. Phys.25,653(1953), distinguishes fixed-position and particle-following averages and treats the mechanical force. The prototype derives its specified closed-cavity traction directly from mass and momentum conservation rather than transferring a formula from an open beam or a moving reflector.
