# Pulse-block displacement, revision 1

Author-only. The source has the exact optical dispersion and energy-centroid transit response of a lossless matched block. Its completed forward model treats canonical polariton momentum as the kinetic wave momentum paired with whole-body mechanical translation. The missing distinction is physical momentum assignment, not phase/group differentiation, pulse normalization, interface algebra, or parameter fitting. No model difficulty is claimed before frozen evaluation.

## Closed apparatus and measured system

The public block has rest response `epsilon_r=mu_r=1+f/[1-(omega+i0)^2]`, with unknown f in[.2,.4]. Positive oscillator strength gives a passive lossless response; the material pole is outside every pulse support. The same electric and magnetic response makes each bare plane face impedance matched to vacuum. No unspecified coating force, external support, absorption or immersed test particle enters. M includes every electric, magnetic and mechanical constituent of the freely suspended body.

The pulse is an asymptotic band-limited scattering state. Its positive-frequency amplitude is proportional to `(1-u^2)^4` for |u|<1, where u=(omega-center)/width. Hence incident energy weights are proportional to `(1-u^2)^8`, not amplitude or photon-number weights. Centers1.5–1.7 and halfwidths.04–.10 place its support within1.4–1.8. The known material pole at1 is unexcited. The body starts at rest and unexcited. Its internal forces are translationally invariant and no external mechanical reservoir acts.

The complete-body center-of-mass position is measured. The small-energy response is extracted at fixed shape, geometry and material before taking the asymptotic late-time limit. Optical fields can therefore be evaluated in the resting undeformed block. Higher-order material heating or recoil cannot produce a secular term in the measured leading coefficient. Elastic energy generated by order-E internal forces is of order E^2; the optical material oscillators have no residual order-E excitation after the asymptotic pulse. This is why the whole-body final readout can be fixed without assigning a local material force density or assuming a coating law.

Units have c=Omega0=epsilon0=mu0=1. Transit is a time; the mechanical coefficient `M*c^2*DeltaX/E` is a length. The public interface defines both directly and gives the limit order. It does not name a momentum prescription, center-of-energy formula, or required numerical method. The exact neutral instruction permits replacing every implementation helper while preserving the API. The unknown starts as None and fit is unfinished.

## Derivation and completed source

Set `n=1+f/(1-omega^2)` and `ng=d(omega*n)/domega`. Maxwell matching gives a transmitted spectral phase `exp(i*omega*n*L)` and zero reflection. The transit energy centroid is

    transit = L * energy_average[ng].

Both controls evaluate this exactly with 96-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over the known pulse support. They include the complete group response and therefore the energy stored in electric and magnetic material oscillators.

For a monochromatic normal mode the canonical polariton momentum is `n*U/c`. This is a legitimate translation-generator momentum; it is not the kinetic momentum of optical energy used with the product M times body velocity. In dispersive media it also differs from the bare field integral of D cross B. The source uses this canonical assignment with the exact residence time `ng*L/c`, giving

    source displacement coefficient = L * energy_average[(1-n)*ng].

After the pulse has left, compare its energy centroid with the same pulse traversing vacuum. Conservation of the isolated system's center of energy gives the compensating whole-body shift,

    physical displacement coefficient = L * energy_average[ng-1].

These are not two consistent bookkeeping conventions for the same mechanical prediction. A consistent canonical balance would change the corresponding material momentum too. The source instead pairs canonical wave momentum with the body's mechanical velocity. It conserves its assumed total momentum but fails the required whole-system energy-center motion.

Throughout the specified frequency and f range,0<n<1 and ng>1. Thus both predictions are positive. The diagnostic is a difference in magnitude, not an immediate sign contradiction. The earlier below-resonance exploration, whose source moved backward, is preserved but not used. A simpler phase-index approximation to optical energy transport was rejected because it closely repeats archived resonator-energy; the final source retains group transport correctly.
