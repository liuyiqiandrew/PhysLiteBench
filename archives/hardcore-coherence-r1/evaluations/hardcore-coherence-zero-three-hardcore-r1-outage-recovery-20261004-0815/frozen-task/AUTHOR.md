# Hard-core boson coherence, revision 1

This task distinguishes exact propagation of site occupations from the physical one-body operator measured after release. The apparatus is an isolated open chain of hard-core bosons, initially a specified number-state product. Its hopping and potential are exact model inputs. At the readout time the lattice and interactions are removed suddenly; subsequent collisionless expansion preserves the initial momentum distribution. Dividing the known single-orbital envelope leaves the bosonic interference profile. Keeping interactions during expansion would define a different experiment, so their removal is a necessary apparatus fact.

The completed source solves the entire one-particle hopping problem without approximation. If P contains the three propagated occupied orbitals, it uses rho_ij=sum_alpha conjugate(P_i,alpha) P_j,alpha for both density and momentum. The open-chain hard-core boson mapping makes every site density and nearest-neighbor coherence exactly equal to this fermionic result. The kinetic energy, total number, positivity and unitary dynamics are correct. Off-diagonal bosonic correlations between more distant sites contain the occupation-dependent ordering operator between the two endpoints. Replacing those correlations by the fermionic one-body matrix changes the momentum image. This is a wrong physical operator identification after a correct state propagation, rather than a failed diagonalization or a Wick-sign arithmetic error.

For the private oracle, form one augmented orbital matrix P^(i) for each site i: change the sign of occupied-orbital rows below i and append the unit column at i. The determinant

    G_ij = det[(P^(i))^dagger P^(j)]

is <b_i b_j^dagger>. Thus rho_ij=G_ji off the diagonal and rho_ii=1-G_ii. With v_j=exp(-i q x_j), the readout is v^dagger rho v/8. This fixes the signed wave-number convention and the per-site normalization. Averaging over one Brillouin interval gives 3/8 in both models.

The independent verifier builds the fixed-three-particle hard-core boson Hamiltonian directly in occupation states, with bosonic matrix elements and no string mapping. It propagates the state, applies b_i^dagger b_j directly and evaluates the physical detector. There is no Hilbert-space truncation. Across 72 corner/random controls, the determinant oracle and this reference differ by at most 5.21e-14. An additional direct many-body ODE agrees with the reference eigensolver to 1.26e-12. Checks cover both hopping endpoints, trap/tilt corners, zero and maximum duration, signed momenta, time-reversal density and momentum identities, Hermiticity, positive one-body matrices, nonnegative momentum profiles, particle number, energy and exact density/nearest-bond equivalence.

Primary background is Rigol and Muramatsu, [Emergence of quasi-condensates of hard-core bosons at finite momentum](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0403387), PRL93,230404(2004), which treats evolution from a Fock state and the resulting momentum correlations, and their [Ground-state properties of hard-core bosons confined on one-dimensional optical lattices](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0409132), PRA72,013604(2005), for the one-body density-matrix framework. The finite task is derived from its explicit Hamiltonian and preparation, rather than numerical results copied from either paper.

The broad archive scan and reviewed comparisons are in results/archive-overlap-audit.json. Related archived tasks cover Bogoliubov thermal depletion versus total momentum variance, bosonic transfer counting noise, canonical normalization of paired modes, and multiphoton distinguishability phases. They share quantum statistics but do not test the occupation-dependent relation between a hard-core boson release operator and its exact fermionic propagation.
