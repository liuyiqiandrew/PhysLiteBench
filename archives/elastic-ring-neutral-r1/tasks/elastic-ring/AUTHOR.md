# Elastic ring, revision 1

This candidate tests a statistical-mechanical limit that calibration does not observe. The finite Cartesian spring system equilibrates before its radial fluctuations shrink. The source instead uses the canonical ensemble of the exactly constrained rhombus, with the correct mass metric for that ensemble. Its angular energy and all apparatus controls are otherwise correct.

For centered unit-side rhombi, write the adjacent side vectors as u and v with relative angle psi. The four positions are (−u−v, u−v, u+v, −u+v)/2. Overall rotation and psi have kinetic Gram matrix [[2,1],[1,1]], whose determinant is 1. The completed shortcut computes this matrix from Cartesian tangents rather than guessing a uniform angle density.

In the stated experiment, the four bond-extension fluctuations must also be integrated. Let C contain the Cartesian gradients of the four side lengths. The normal spring Hessian is C transpose C. Its four positive eigenvalues have product 16 sin(psi)^2. Integrating these Gaussian coordinates therefore leaves an angular weight proportional to exp(−U/T)/sin(psi). The hard angular walls make the distribution normalizable and stay fixed as K grows. Fixing the center of mass and integrating the free orientation only contribute constants. No angular-momentum constraint is imposed.

The independent verifier builds the Cartesian normal Hessian and tangent metric numerically, then uses different angular quadrature. A second check performs finite-K quadrature in the four side lengths and psi. The circle-intersection construction has Cartesian Jacobian l1*l2*l3*l4/(d*h), where d is the diagonal joining beads 1 and 3 and h is the height of bead2 above that diagonal. This calculation approaches the same distribution without imposing rigid constraints or inserting the limiting angular weight. The validator also checks that Jacobian by differentiating the eight-coordinate Cartesian map.

The zero-temperature torque transducer measures only the angular element, with torque −stiffness*sin(angle−preferred). It does not measure an equilibrium clamp reaction. This identifies the one stiffness with no vibrational entropy contribution. Both completed controls give identical calibration predictions. The public sigma is a fixed 0.005 for all 144 observations; it does not encode a noiseless response. Calibration seed 34021 and noise seed 44021 are fixed.

The three private groups measure bounded angular moments at symmetric and biased preferred angles. The absolute RMSE threshold 0.025 is a 2.5-percentage-point moment accuracy requirement. It is much larger than discretization and calibration propagation errors. It is unchanged across all three groups and both controls; there is no implementation inspection.

Scientific validation passes 256 independent calibration noise draws. The checked-in fit is 0.898597 for true stiffness 0.9, with reduced chi-square 0.999876. Oracle hidden errors are at most 0.000157, while the completed rigid-metric control gives 0.131–0.142. The normal-Hessian reference agrees with the oracle within 2.3e−14. Finite-K Cartesian quadrature at K/T=1e8 differs from the limiting result by at most 5.3e−6 at the screened corners, decreasing by approximately a factor 10 per decade of stiffness. Increasing both radial and angular quadrature changes the final result by at most 1.3e−14. Local isolated verification gives 7 passes for the oracle and 4 passes plus the 3 intended prediction failures for the shortcut.
