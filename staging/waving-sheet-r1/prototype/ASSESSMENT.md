# Waving-sheet pumping prototype

This is a new candidate outline and scientific prototype, with no task package or model trials. It was developed while survivor-transport revision 1 was awaiting its final peer review, before any survivor model outcome.

An anchored infinite sheet has material coordinates `x=X`, `y=a*cos(k*X-omega*t)`. Its material points have no horizontal velocity; the sheet is extensible. An incompressible Newtonian liquid occupies the upper half-space, with no slip at the sheet, no imposed mean pressure gradient, and no distant imposed shear or counterflow. The periodic stationary solution has decaying oscillatory fields and bounded mean velocity. Take the stationary limit before the far-field limit. The apparatus measures first-harmonic fluid velocities at specified heights and the far-field mean pumping speed. The measured coefficients are extrapolated as amplitude a tends to zero at fixed frequency, wave number and viscosity: first harmonic divided by a, and mean speed divided by a squared. This removes finite-amplitude truncation ambiguity without prescribing a solution method.

The one unknown is kinematic viscosity nu in [.7,1.4] square millimeters per second. Proposed controls use wave number [.7,1.3] inverse millimeters and frequency [.5,8] inverse seconds. Calibration would contain signed real and imaginary first-harmonic velocities at multiple heights and frequencies. Source and oracle agree exactly on the complete linear response for every viscosity, while that response identifies viscosity. Instrument errors will be fixed by channel units if a package is authorized.

The proposed source solves the unsteady Stokes problem correctly and includes the quadratic moving-boundary contribution to pumping. It omits convective momentum transport from the mean fluid equation. Small displacement makes convection higher order than the oscillatory velocity, but the requested pumping itself starts at that higher order. This is a physical approximation applied inconsistently to the order of the observable, not a wrong root, sign or unfinished prediction. The pumping readout is far from the oscillatory layer, so this candidate does not also require a correction from Eulerian to Lagrangian velocity.

For the complex convention `exp(i*(k*x-omega*t))`, define `s=sqrt(k²-i*omega/nu)` with positive real part. The first-order streamfunction divided by a is `psi=A*exp(-k*y)+B*exp(-s*y)`, with `A=omega*s/[k*(s-k)]`, `B=-omega/(s-k)`. It obeys `psi(0)=omega/k`, `psi'(0)=0`. The horizontal and vertical amplitudes are `u=psi'`, `v=-i*k*psi`. The exact geometric wall condition supplies

    U(0)/a² = -Re(psi''(0))/2 = omega*Re(s)/2.

This is the complete source pumping value. The averaged Navier-Stokes momentum equation instead gives

    nu*U''/a² = Re(v*conjugate(u'))/2,
    U'(infinity) = 0,
    U(infinity)/a² = U(0)/a² + integral(Re(u*conjugate(v))/2, y=0..infinity)/nu.

The oracle prototype evaluates the exponential integral analytically. Its independent reference solves the fourth-order linear vorticity boundary ODE, then a separate inhomogeneous mean-momentum boundary ODE using the actual period-averaged convective acceleration. It does not insert the exponential amplitudes or integrated pumping formula. Twelve domain cases agree within 1.86e-9 relatively; linear velocities agree within 2.47e-11. Increasing the depth from20/k to26/k and tightening the BVP tolerance changes pumping by less than7.16e-10. Twenty-five noiseless calibration recoveries span the viscosity interval and err by at most4.86e-9. Both controls approach Taylor's `U/(a²*omega*k)=1/2` as frequency tends to zero. At k=nu=1, frequency2 gives source .6360 and physical .4465 after normalization by a²*omega; frequency8 gives1.0643 versus .3674. These are positive ordinary pumping signals, with no detector cancellation. Full noise validation, energy/momentum checks and a final public-input review remain future package work.

The archive scan found no prior waving-sheet or convective-streaming candidate. The closest actual AUTHOR records read were acoustic-cavity-pressure, pressure-surface-waves, thermal-unsteady-sphere and unsteady-sphere. Acoustic-cavity-pressure concerns fixed-mass compressible density redistribution and a wall force; this proposal concerns incompressible mean momentum transported by a finite-frequency boundary flow. Pressure-surface-waves concerns follower traction in an elastic solid. The sphere tasks concern linear fluid impedance or thermal-force spectra. The shared broad theme is using a valid lower-order field for an incorrectly closed higher-order observable; no claim of a wholly unrelated mathematical principle is made.

Primary background: [E. O. Tuck, A note on a swimming problem, JFM31,305–308 (1968)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022112068000169). Root read the publisher abstract, which identifies the missing convective contribution in Reynolds' earlier calculation. The formulas and boundary ODE above were derived and checked independently here; the full paper was not available through the opened publisher page. [Sauzade, Elfring and Lauga, Taylor's swimming sheet: Analysis and improvement of the perturbation series](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.4029) supplies related small-amplitude sheet background, not the finite-inertia correction.
