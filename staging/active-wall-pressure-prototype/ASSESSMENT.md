# Active wall pressure prototype

Unevaluated. No model runs or canonical task changes.

The proposed apparatus is a dilute ideal gas of active Brownian particles in two dimensions. A channel is homogeneous along y and confined in x by two soft, stationary, orientation-dependent wall coatings. The complete translational and rotational Langevin equations specify both the normal force and the angular drift from a single potential. Pressure is the coating's mechanical reaction per wall length. The common particle number per unit wall length is unknown; all mobilities, propulsion speeds, temperatures and wall controls are known.

The source solves the full stationary joint position/orientation equation, including every wall parameter. It then applies the torque-free midplane stress formula. Calibration uses orientation-independent coatings, at several speeds, temperatures and separations. The source and physical readout agree exactly throughout that continuum calibration, including finite-channel anisotropy. Hidden coatings also orient particles, so mechanical pressure differs even though the source's stationary density is correct.

Write normalized joint density psi, rho=integral psi dtheta, m=integral cos(theta) psi dtheta, and q=integral cos(theta)^2 psi dtheta. With translational mobility one, zero stationary mass flux gives integral_0^infinity V_x psi = T rho(0)+v integral_0^infinity m dx. The first angular moment gives Dr integral m = v q(0)-T m'(0)-integral sin(theta) tau psi dx, where tau=-(Dr/T)V_theta. Thus the missing term is physical transfer through the wall's orienting torque, not an error in solving either stationary operator. Translation of the right coating changes V by -V_x times its displacement, so the force integral includes the spatial derivative of the orientational energy as well as the scalar repulsion.

The first prototype used a quadratic repulsion and s^2/(ell^2+s^2) alignment. Its scripts and reports are preserved. A smoother quartic repulsion k s^4/4 and s^4/(ell^4+s^4) alignment reduces grid-interface errors and increases the useful separation without changing the mechanism. Here s=max(abs(x)-L,0).

`quartic_check.py` uses a positive Scharfetter-Gummel position/angle Markov generator. `spectral_quartic.py` independently evolves angular Fourier moments with centered conservative spatial flux. Both calculate the direct wall force and the midplane stress. The exact integrated moment identity above supplies a third independently derived readout. Refinement is required before a task freeze; the existing prototype supports ~22–39% relative pressure differences at h=1,2,-1 and an exact h=0 calibration identity. No empirical model difficulty has been established.

Primary source: Solon et al., "Pressure is not a state function for generic active fluids," Nature Physics 11, 673–678 (2015), DOI 10.1038/nphys3377, https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3952. The paper establishes the dependence of active mechanical pressure on wall torques. The finite-channel formula and common-potential wall model here were derived explicitly above.
