## Family progression and the limits of incremental hardening

The actual r1 and r2 author files and trial ledgers were read. R1 supplied exact positional transport but omitted all kinetic heat: 2/3 plain passes and 3/3 hinted passes. R2 supplied the isotropic kinetic term but averaged distinct drag channels: 1/3 plain and 3/3 hinted. R3 supplied anisotropic zero-field heat and full Hall positional transport, then measured field-dependent cubic relaxation. Its original 0/3 plain and 3/3 hinted evidence remains historical; the fresh neutral batch above is 2/3. R4 preserved the exact mean but changed the observable to long-time variance; the fresh 2/3 result remains archived. None of these batches is pooled or replaced by an earlier favorable result.

For r3, changing a drag ratio, field strength, dimension or thermal Fourier profile leaves the physical bottleneck as the velocity Poisson problem already explicitly represented by the passers. A non-Gaussian bath would be a substantive apparatus change, but it would require its own physical calorimeter/reservoir definition; simply modifying an OU collision polynomial would not establish a fair entropy task. For r4, a higher cumulant or a fuller counting eigenproblem reuses the method already visible in both correct submissions. More basis modes would increase numerical work without establishing a different failed physical assumption.

## Closest alternatives checked against actual archives

- **Reservoir-labelled entropy versus aggregate path entropy.** The actual chemical-route-power source computes exact aggregate Markov entropy, while its oracle counts separate reservoir regeneration work. Equal affinities give exact nonzero calibration. All three fresh trials corrected the route assignment. Replacing those chemical routes with parallel Brownian baths would retain the same aggregate-kinetics/reservoir-resolution distinction. Magnetic-bath-transfer also supplied exact Gaussian mechanics and tested reservoir heat attribution; all three submissions used the correct thermostat power. A bath relabeling is not recommended as an entropy r5.
- **Memory or moving-bath heat.** Thermal-bodies r11's three passes rebuilt the full coloured-bath covariance. Active-bath-work's three passes derived the joint fast velocity–force covariance. Rotating-reservoir r2 now changes the physical contact location and finite connector strain work, while keeping the old relative-velocity correction. Reusing that mechanism for entropy would overlap the live hardening revision rather than add an independent constraint.
- **Finite-window stored energy or endpoint-sensitive tails.** Thermal-bodies r12's actual author derivation and three trial reviews show that all submissions corrected outer-thermostat heat by including intervening stored energy, with exact endpoint/current correlations. R4 already states that finite-mass kinetic endpoint variables need not preserve the full exponential-moment domain even though they preserve the requested first two long-time rates. A tail or SCGF-domain experiment is scientifically different from those rates, but there is no validated ordinary finite observable, exact shared calibration, stable hidden margin or practical independent reference in this review. It remains a research question, not a proposed task. Finite-window variance alone would repeat r12; choosing singular counting parameters to create a numerical failure would not address the goal.

## Decision

Keep current r3 and archived r4 unchanged. Do not spend another three-trial batch on coefficient, cumulant-order, bath-label or finite-window variants of these solved laws. Resume entropy-family development only with a concrete new reservoir/preparation/measurement constraint and an independent finite-observable reference. At present there is no qualified r5 proposal or numerical prototype to hand off. The evidence supports prioritizing the already distinct rotating-reservoir hardening while preserving all entropy outcomes.
