# Three materials and thermal outlines

Author-only outlines, 2026-10-04. No task implementation, numerical prototype, scientific sweep or model evaluation was run for these proposals. Analytic examples establish useful signal scales; they do not establish full-domain margins or empirical difficulty. Existing tasks and outcomes are unchanged.

## 1. Fixed-number band calorimetry — preferred minimal prototype

**Apparatus.** A macroscopic, noninteracting spinless fermion solid has equal numbers of one-particle states in two flat bands at energies −W/2 and +W/2. The thermodynamic limit is part of the ideal model. Its known filling n is prepared before electrical isolation. A weak thermal contact allows complete canonical equilibration, including redistribution between bands, while preserving total particle number. Take equilibration before neglecting the contact energy. At fixed geometry, a differential calorimeter measures heat per temperature increment per available one-particle state, with the empty supporting material subtracted. Units are a declared energy E0, temperature E0/kB and heat capacity kB per state. W/E0 in [.8,1.2] is the sole unknown.

**Completed approximation.** Solve the exact Fermi occupations and the chemical potential that gives the specified n. Then use the open-system thermal susceptibility at that state, C_mu=M2/T², where Mj is the average of (E−mu)^j f(1−f) over all band states. This is positive and invariant under a common energy-origin shift. The wrong model does not omit the filling or solve its chemical potential incorrectly; it treats the infinitesimal calorimetric response as permitting number fluctuations even though the prepared device is closed.

**Shared calibration and identifiability.** At n=1/2, symmetry gives mu=0 and M1=0. Both responses are exactly C=x² sech²(x), x=W/(4T). Use several temperatures T=.10–.15 E0/kB, so x≥4/3 throughout the unknown range. Its logarithmic derivative with W has sign 1−x tanh(x)<0. Every calibration curve is therefore strictly decreasing and identifies W globally; a curve at one temperature is already injective. Several temperatures give a substantive caloric calibration.

**Extrapolation and reference.** Fixed-N calorimetry instead gives C_N=(M2−M1²/M0)/T². This is the number constraint on the response, not a change of equilibrium populations. At n=.3 and W/T=log(9), the exact occupations are (.5,.1). C_mu=9 log²(9)/200, C_N=9 log²(9)/272≈.160, a 36% relative excess with an ordinary nonzero physical signal. A neighborhood retains a substantial margin; its quantitative full-range extent remains to be checked. An independent reference can sum the finite canonical partition function over the number in the upper band, with binomial multiplicities and fixed total number, then take the per-state thermodynamic limit. Direct re-equilibrated mean-energy differences at fixed n provide a second check. Neither reference needs the susceptibility subtraction.

**Overlap and risk.** The actual quantum-calorimetry author tests attached-bath energy versus bare subsystem energy; finite-band-reservoir tests quench-created bound-mode occupations; finite-heat-reservoir tests fixed total energy; spin-strain tests quantum versus equal-time response. This proposal keeps the same equilibrium one-particle populations and changes the particle-number constraint during a classical thermodynamic derivative. There is a broad shared constrained-response theme, not an exact archived duplicate. The two-band model is deliberately simple; an expert may repair it immediately. Primary background on canonical fermion calorimetry: [Kuzmenko and Mikhajlov, arXiv:0811.2078](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.2078). Only its primary abstract was read; the formulas and two-band example above are independently derived, not attributed to that paper.

## 2. Anisotropic crystal surface diffusion — preferred continuum alternative

**Apparatus.** A stress-free crystalline surface is uniform in one transverse direction and periodic along its mean tangent. Surface diffusion is the only material transport; no evaporation, deposition or bulk diffusion occurs. Its known free energy per actual area is gamma(theta)=gamma0[1+epsilon cos(4theta)], theta being the surface-normal angle in crystal coordinates. Isothermal isotropic surface mobility is unknown and positive. Prepare an infinitesimal sinusoidal height perturbation of known wavenumber about a planar crystallographic cut theta0; measure its positive decay rate. A combined mobility B with units m6/(J s) makes B gamma0 q4 a rate.

**Completed approximation.** Use the actual gamma(theta) in a local isotropic capillary chemical potential and solve conservative surface diffusion. This treats orientation-dependent surface energy as an orientation-dependent scalar tension. It uses all controls and preserves volume. The true virtual work of the specified orientation-dependent area energy uses surface stiffness. This is a constitutive approximation, not a discretization or stability error.

**Calibration, gap and reference.** Linear decay rates are B gamma0 q4[1+epsilon cos(4theta0)] in that approximation and B gamma0 q4[1−15epsilon cos(4theta0)] physically. At theta0=pi/8, both agree exactly for nonzero epsilon, and multiple q identify B linearly and globally. At epsilon=.04, cuts theta0=0 and pi/4 give physical factors .4 and1.6 versus source1.04 and.96. All orientations remain stable because the stiffness is at least .4 gamma0. The ordinary rates differ by160% and40%, respectively. An independent reference should perturb the full graph-area energy and take its discrete Hessian, then evolve the mass-conserving surface flux; it must not insert the analytic stiffness. Anisotropic mobility is explicitly absent so energy and mobility cannot be confounded.

**Overlap and risk.** Coherent-alloy tests bulk elastic compatibility; capillary-mixture tests hydrodynamic stress; nematic-wall-torque tests boundary work of a divergence term with an unchanged fixed-trace state. Here surface orientation changes the local area energy and hence a conserved shape-relaxation rate. No exact surface-stiffness candidate was found in the searched history. The repair is a well-known variational derivative, so difficulty is uncertain. Primary support: [The role of the interface stiffness tensor on grain boundary dynamics (2018)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1359645418304932). The indexed publisher text explicitly supplies the surface-energy/stiffness distinction; full publisher retrieval returned403. This supports the constitutive issue, not any proposed benchmark outcome.

## 3. Degenerate-state calorimetry — reserve outline

**Apparatus.** Each isolated molecular unit has one state A of energy0 and g distinct states B_j of energy−E. E, g and the bath temperature are known. Each microscopic A→B_j rate is nu exp[E/(2T)] and reverse rate nu exp[−E/(2T)], with one unknown positive attempt frequency nu. These explicit rates define a weak thermal Markov bath, with no chemical or mechanical work during observation. Each experiment starts in A; the preparation cost is outside the calorimeter gate. Measure the accumulated heat delivered to the bath per molecule.

**Completed approximation and calibration.** Keep the exact lumped two-state population evolution, including the factor g in the forward rate. Assign T log(k_forward/k_reverse)=E+T log g to each net A→B event. This is a complete mesostate free-energy-drop heat attribution, rather than an incorrect population solver. Population relaxation across several E,T,g settings strictly identifies nu: p_B(t)=p_eq[1−exp(−nu L t)]. Heat observations at g=1 also agree exactly. Hidden g>1 heat is E p_B(t), whereas the approximation is (E+T log g)p_B(t). At E=T and g=2 the relative discrepancy is log2≈69%, independent of waiting time away from zero signal.

**Reference, overlap and risk.** A full(g+1)-state energy-tagged master equation gives an independent reference. Competitive-adsorption and sorption-transport omit shared site occupancy; trapped-excitation-heat omits an energy-transport channel. This task keeps exact kinetics but attributes intrinsic degeneracy entropy to released heat. It shares general thermodynamic bookkeeping with several archived observables and may be especially easy once the explicit microscopic energies are read. Primary support: [Seifert, Stochastic thermodynamics of single enzymes and molecular motors, EPJ E34,26(2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.3119), whose abstract distinguishes internal energy, intrinsic entropy and free energy before identifying heat. No nonisothermal force law or unspecified reservoir constraint is assumed.

## Recommendation

Prototype fixed-number band calorimetry first if a minimal thermal constraint test is wanted; its exact calibration, finite canonical reference and analytic36% gap are unusually clean. Surface diffusion is the stronger continuum alternative and has larger stable gaps. Keep degenerate-state heat in reserve because its microscopic energy balance is likely obvious. None should be packaged or evaluated from these outlines alone. Required next work is a bounded-domain/reference prototype and an independent source/convention review, with ordinary fixed instrument noise and the unchanged4% gate if a task is later authorized.
