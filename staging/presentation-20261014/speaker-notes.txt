SLIDE 2 / 55s
The benchmark separates parameter fitting from physical extrapolation. The agent receives an apparatus description, calibration observations, a starter program and public tests. The hidden evaluator checks the fit and parameter, but also asks for behavior beyond the calibration regime. A successful numerical fit can therefore coexist with an incorrect physical model. The thirteen tasks include transport, stochastic dynamics, constrained fluctuations and quantum or statistical systems. Private tests, reference solutions and physics hints stay outside the ordinary agent image. One important limitation is task selection: the roster was developed against GPT-5.6 Luna. We should interpret this as a deliberately challenging selected suite, not a random sample of all physics problems. A historical zero out of three selection result is also not a permanent guarantee of failure.

The stacked bars aggregate three complete effort settings per model, so every row contains one hundred seventeen attempts. Green means normal completion with a pass. Gold means the agent completed, fit calibration and the parameter correctly, and failed only hidden predictions. Almost every normally completed failure is in that category: one hundred eighty-six of one hundred ninety-one, or ninety-seven point four percent. At least one hundred forty-one of those also show a public-test pass in the tool record. This is strong evidence that fitting and public tests do not validate the extrapolated model. DeepSeek has a different dominant bottleneck: one hundred five execution errors in one hundred seventeen attempts, including eighty-three timeouts. Only twelve attempts complete normally, and those twelve fail predictions. A zero pass rate therefore combines physical errors with failures to deliver a completed solution. We must separate those causes before drawing conclusions about physics ability.

SLIDE 7 / 80s
This case makes the aggregate pattern concrete. In one GPT-6 Luna Low run, the model reads the apparatus, constructs a friction scan, implements a weighted least-squares fit and runs the public tests. Both public tests pass. The saved source shows that the physical predictor is unchanged. In particular, the first correction to the conditional contact population remains identically zero. This matters because equal stationary probabilities for the valve do not guarantee equal position-conditioned contact residence in the finite-mass correction. The final program fits the observed parameter but misses that contribution to the extrapolated entropy response. The largest hidden-group error is seventy-seven point four percent. To check that this is the relevant omission, the diagnostic analysis holds the fitted friction fixed and restores only the population correction in an in-memory copy. The largest tested error then drops below five hundredths of a percent. That intervention supports the failure location in this saved program. It is an analyst diagnosis, not a new model success, and the original reward remains unchanged.

SLIDE 8 / 70s
The same diagnostic procedure was applied to four selected failures. First, it reproduced the saved errors. Then it changed one identified physical ingredient while holding the fitted parameter fixed. For entropy, that is the conditional contact population. For magnetic tracer, it is the mixed diffusion term that couples longitudinal motion and detector noise. For single-file memory, it is the two-time covariance appropriate to deterministic preparation. For reaction–diffusion, it is the charge-conditioned initial covariance instead of independent constituent counts. All tested groups fall below their original tolerances after the correction. This makes the physical diagnosis stronger than a qualitative reading of the source alone. The scope remains limited: these are four chosen cases, and the reaction–diffusion intervention checks only the fluctuation groups. It does not rerun the unchanged mean-transport solver. No benchmark result or submitted source was altered, and these repairs are not additional model passes.

SLIDE 11 / 60s
The completed experiments support three conclusions. First, model choice and configured effort create a wide cost–success tradeoff on this frozen suite. The observed frontier reaches high success with GPT-6 Sol Low and High, while lower-cost Luna settings trade away substantial success. Second, a completed program can fit calibration and pass public tests while preserving an inappropriate physical approximation. The trajectory analysis identifies concrete examples, and controlled diagnostic corrections support four of those diagnoses. Third, execution reliability is a separate component of benchmark success, especially in the DeepSeek results and in the Astra Extra High timeouts. These distinctions are useful for interpreting the pending hint study. A gain from a physics hint would be most informative if it changed the final physical implementation, rather than only increasing tool use or time. The current data do not yet tell us whether that happens. The result remains open until the comparison is complete.
