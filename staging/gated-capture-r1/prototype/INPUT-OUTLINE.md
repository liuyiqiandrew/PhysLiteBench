# Proposed public apparatus

A stationary spherical target of known radius a is surrounded by an infinite three-dimensional dilute solution of independent Brownian particles. The unknown common particle diffusivity D lies in[.7,1.3] in fixed units L0^2/t0. The solvent is at rest, there are no particle-target forces outside the surface, and concentration far from the sphere is maintained at the known constant c_infinity. All surface reaction is irreversible. Products are removed immediately; reaction does not alter target geometry, block sites or change its surface state.

The whole surface has one state G=0 or1. It switches according to a continuous-time Markov process, with known rate k01 from0 to1 and known rate k10 from1 to0, in units1/t0. These rates do not depend on particle positions, arrival events or reaction counts. State changes are spatially uniform and instantaneous.

In state i the coating has known finite intrinsic reaction velocity kappa_i, in units L0/t0. This defines the usual diffusion reaction boundary: when that state is held, the local removal flux per surface area is kappa_i times the concentration immediately outside the surface. Unreacted particles are reflected. The same local reaction rule applies while the coating switches, with no stored surface population or delayed reaction step. Zero reactivity means reflection.

The combined particle distribution and surface process have reached their stationary state under the maintained far reservoir before the reading. The measured output is the long-time mean number of particles absorbed by this one sphere per unit time, counting each absorbed particle once.

Candidate controls: radius .6–1.8; reaction velocities0–15; each switching rate .03–1.5; fixed known reservoir concentration1 in units L0^-3. Output is in units1/t0. Equal-reactivity calibration will vary radius, reaction velocity and both nonzero transition rates, without identifying that equality as a special case in apparatus prose. The final README would document the data/API and fixed instrument uncertainty without supplying a homogenized or corrected flux formula.
