# Pulse-family bounded numerical study

There were no failed scientific attempts, domain changes, clock changes or post-result threshold changes. Both the exact executed source and plan are preserved with the log and every result row.

The source and physical model use the same retarded electric-sheet oscillator, radiation reaction, pulse, spring dynamics and calibration. They differ only in assigning `-P*e_dot` or `P_dot*e` to mechanical acceleration. The independent reference evolves the sheet oscillator through Maxwell characteristic jumps, evaluates force from the two exterior E/B stresses, and integrates both Newton equations. It never uses either compact force expression as mechanical input. The oracle instead reconstructs the causal fields in frequency space and integrates the mechanical Green function. The incident spectrum is real and has zero phase, the analytic incident energy is one, the area is one, and the finite clock is measured from the incident energy centroid. Calibration records use positive forward radiation load in a separate held-sheet preparation.

All 40 independent reference cases agree with the oracle to a maximum scaled discrepancy of 1.20e-7. Twelve cases have separately refined time resolution and tail interval; the largest change is 1.20e-7. Extending the oracle interval also refines its frequency spacing, so that result is explicitly a combined tail/spectral-period check. These errors are far below the unchanged 0.04 prediction gate. The largest optical energy-balance error is 1.51e-10, mechanical work/energy error 2.98e-11, and impulse-versus-reflected-energy error 1.46e-10. Source and physical complete-pulse impulses differ by at most 4.25e-11.

The predeclared instantaneous checks were completed from all 40 saved reference readout states by `audit_saved_states.py`, using separate direct spectral quadrature for the incident field. The largest jump, stress/Lorentz, energy-rate or canonical/kinetic identity residual is 1.39e-17. This is a follow-through check of the saved study, not another domain survey or ODE execution. It does not turn a leading-order stationary-field calculation into a finite-amplitude moving-body simulation.

The full survey contains 960 tensor cases and 128 seeded interiors. Every physical and source signal exceeds 0.05 in magnitude; the smallest physical signal is 0.11148 and the smallest source signal is 0.14832. No signal changes sign between the models. **Forty-eight individual cases have a discrepancy below 4%, with a minimum of 2.5893%.** All are retained unchanged in `domain-survey.json`. There is no uniform-domain gap claim or proof between sampled points.

The 27 diagnostic rows were fixed before the survey. They form three groups of nine, each spanning three widths, three mechanical ratios and three prescribed clocks. Across the 41 declared strengths, the smallest group RMS is 0.71396 and the smallest source group error is 4.5509%. This is a modest margin above the 4% gate. No weak row was dropped to obtain it.

Stationary calibration agrees for the two force laws to 1.11e-16. The noiseless map is strictly monotone in positive strength; 41 fitted strengths have maximum absolute error 2.43e-9. Calibration uses 144 records with fixed sigma 0.0005. The fixed dataset fits f=0.30987737 with reduced chi-square 0.81838. Its source group errors are 5.7097%, 6.5295% and 7.1578%; the largest physical-model error is 0.07536%.

All 256 independently drawn calibration datasets pass parameter and calibration checks; all physical predictions pass and all three source groups fail for every dataset. There are 128 datasets at f=0.31 and 128 at the predeclared strength grid, including endpoints. The same dataset and fitted parameter are used by each source/physical pair; these are 256 datasets, not 512 independent fits. The worst parameter error is 0.12124%, worst physical prediction error 0.23036%, and smallest source group error 4.5113%. Raw calibration values and all outcomes are saved.

Zero strength gives zero force, and an uncoupled inert mass remains at rest. Small mechanical frequencies give the expected quadratic displacement scaling (ratio 3.99798 when frequency doubles). The prescribed large-frequency cases obey the rigid-response bound. Scaling the incident amplitude by 0.5 and 2 preserves the normalized weak-pulse readout within 7.60e-10. These are checks of the weak-drive equations, not evidence for their use at finite recoil.

The slowest base reference case took 2.89 seconds. Full reference validation with refinements consumed 167.83 seconds of the 182.95-second study. This study did not time a packaged verifier, public solver or complete 27-row private harness, so compliance with the 60-second verifier limit remains unproved. A task package would also need direct reference validation of its actual graded rows and its final public instruction and image. The substantial optical-force overlap and Luna's earlier successful Lorentz-force derivations remain reasons for caution about empirical difficulty. The spring itself is only a known detector filter.
