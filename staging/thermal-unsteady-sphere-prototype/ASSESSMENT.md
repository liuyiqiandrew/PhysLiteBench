# Thermal unsteady sphere: prototype

Status: scientific prototype only. No task has been packaged and no model has
been evaluated. Independent physics review of the actual source and report passed; no numerical rerun was requested. This is a proposed thermal
revision of the archived unsteady-sphere family, not a new unrelated principle.

## Apparatus and readout

A rigid sphere translates in an unbounded, incompressible Newtonian fluid at
uniform equilibrium temperature. The fluid has density 1000 kg/m³; the particle
has density 2200 kg/m³. The no-slip surface carries the surrounding fluid with
the particle. There is no trap, nearby boundary, external flow, particle heating,
or separate stochastic force source. Gravity is compensated. Fluid and particle
inertia are retained, and convective inertia is negligible. The specified
continuum applies only in the stated finite-frequency range.

The proposed unknown is viscosity in [.0007,.0014] Pa s. Radius lies in
[.5,1.5] µm, temperature in [280,320] K, and angular frequency is zero or in
[.2,8] million radians/s. Driven measurements apply a known small force and
read either component of the complex mean velocity using exp(-i omega t).
Thermal measurements turn the force off and read the stationary spectrum of
one Cartesian particle-velocity component:

    Svv(omega) = integral_-infinity^infinity
                  exp(i omega t) <v(t) v(0)> dt.

This is a two-sided angular-frequency spectrum, with SI units m²/s. A final
interface can report µm²/s using a fixed 10^12 conversion. It is neither a
position spectrum nor a one-sided spectrum per hertz. The finite-frequency
readout does not require a claim about microscopic infinite-frequency
equipartition. Free position diffusion causes no ambiguity in this stationary
velocity measurement in a translation-invariant fluid.

The maximum omega*a/c is .008 using sound speed 1500 m/s. The driven Reynolds
bound is 1.08e-5 for force 1e-13 N. A conservative thermal speed based on bare
particle mass gives Reynolds number below .00140. hbar*omega/(kBT) is below
2.19e-7. These checks support the linear, classical, incompressible continuum
as a finite-frequency effective model; they do not extend it to arbitrary
frequencies in real water.

## Completed alternative and calibration

Both models retain the exact deterministic unsteady Stokes impedance and
particle inertia:

    s = a sqrt(-i omega rho/eta), Re(s) >= 0
    Z = 6 pi eta a (1+s+s²/9)
    mu = 1/(Z-i omega m).

The alternative is a causal stable linear generalized-Langevin response driven
by an independent white force with spectrum 2 kBT*6 pi eta a. It keeps every
input and predicts positive velocity spectra, but its random forcing is not
the equilibrium forcing generated by the same dissipative fluid. Its result is

    S_source = 2 kBT*6 pi eta a*|mu|².

The physical equilibrium contraction gives

    S_physical = 2 kBT Re(Z)*|mu|².

The ratio is 1+a sqrt(rho*|omega|/(2 eta)). This is a physical choice of the
noise source, not a change to the correct impedance, a Fourier normalization
mistake, or an arithmetic defect. It agrees at DC but is thermally inconsistent
at finite frequency. Equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation is familiar, so actual
agent difficulty remains uncertain.

Calibration deliberately measures driven mean velocity, including steady and
finite-frequency in-phase and quadrature responses. The prototype has 14
distinct scalar readings repeated eight times: two radii, four frequencies,
with the zero imaginary DC readings omitted. All noiseless calibration values
are exactly shared. DC response is strictly monotone in viscosity and establishes
global identifiability; the dynamic rows also validate the supplied memory and
added-mass response. Instrument sigma=.002 µm/s is fixed, independent of the
unknown or noiseless response. This is a preliminary 32-noise screen, not the
required 256-draw packaged-task validation.

## Independent fluid calculation

The reference never inserts the closed impedance or the fluctuation-dissipation
formula above. Let x=r/a and write unit-speed exterior flow as

    ur=f(x) cos(theta), utheta=-g(x) sin(theta), g=f+x f'/2.

It solves the two decaying exterior radial modes

    f=A/x³+B exp[-s(x-1)](sx+1)/x³

for no slip f(1)=1, f'(1)=0. The pressure is obtained from the irrotational
mode, p=-i omega rho a A cos(theta)/2. Angular Gaussian quadrature of pressure
and viscous traction gives the full complex force and hence mobility. DC uses
the regular steady solution f=3/(2x)-1/(2x³), with pressure
3 eta cos(theta)/(2a).

For the same auxiliary unit translation the nonzero strain components, with
their common factor 1/a suppressed, are

    Drr=f' cos(theta)
    Dtheta,theta=Dphi,phi=-f' cos(theta)/2
    Dr,theta=-(f'+x f''/2) sin(theta)/2.

Local equilibrium fluid stress has covariance

    <Sigma_ij(r,t) Sigma_kl(r',t')>
       =2 kBT eta (delta_ik delta_jl+delta_il delta_jk)
          delta(r-r') delta(t-t').

The incompressible trace part does no work. The reciprocal contraction therefore
gives force-noise power 4 kBT eta integral D:D* dV. The reference independently
integrates the angular and radial strain power, then propagates that force
through the mobility obtained from surface traction. The equality between this
volume dissipation and Re(Z) is checked, rather than assumed by the reference.

## Results

`report.json` records 56 cases: 24 domain corners including DC and 32 random
finite-frequency cases. Volume-noise and closed-spectrum predictions agree
within 1.08e-13 relative error. Independent surface traction agrees within
1.80e-14, and the dissipation identity within 1.08e-13. Tightened radial and
angular quadrature changes the spectrum by at most 2.99e-15. No-slip residual
is below 5.73e-14. Noise spectra are positive, even in frequency, and agree with
the white model at DC.

The finite-frequency white-source error ranges from .1179 to .7819. Physical
spectra range from 4.68e-15 to 1.09e-12 m²/s. No near-zero subtraction defines
the signal. A 501-point calibration profile has one minimum; noiseless viscosity
recovery is accurate to 2.63e-10 relative, endpoint recovery is exact after
checking endpoints, and 32 noise draws have maximum relative error .000121.
The complete prototype ran in .108 seconds, excluding environment startup.

## Archive overlap and evidence

The archive audit searched author files for white force/noise, fluctuating fluid,
and hydrodynamic memory, then read the relevant physical closures. Exact paths
and hashes are in `archive-audit.json`.

- Archived unsteady-sphere omitted deterministic Basset and added-mass terms.
  Here both are exact and tested in dynamic calibration. This is the same sphere
  family with a different stochastic constitutive error.
- Hot Brownian rotation keeps equilibrium frequency-dependent noise exactly,
  but uses a DC temperature average in a spatially heated fluid. The proposed
  experiment is uniform-temperature translation and instead changes the force
  spectrum itself.
- Hydrodynamic fluctuations and gyroscopic noise omit spatial cross-noise or
  imaginary cross-spectral structure. This proposal is a scalar autocorrelation
  with no mode projection or detector delay.
- Thermal-bodies r11 assigns independent copies of local baths to normal modes;
  colored-spin-memory r2 resets system-bath correlations between drive intervals.
  Neither is this equilibrium fluid-noise contraction.
- Hydrodynamic heating r2 changes local irreversible-heat attribution. There is
  no calorimetric readout in this proposal. Rotating reservoir changes the frame
  in which heat is defined; there is no rotation or heat measurement here.

The shared fluctuation-dissipation background is explicit. No prior result is
discarded or recast, and the proposal is not yet evidence of difficulty.

Primary sources inspected:

- Hauge and Martin-Löf, *Fluctuating hydrodynamics and Brownian motion*,
  Journal of Statistical Physics 7,259–281 (1973),
  https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01030307, derives the contraction of local fluid stress
  to generally nonwhite particle forcing.
- Franosch et al., *Resonances arising from hydrodynamic memory in Brownian
  motion—The colour of thermal noise*, https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.6161, measures
  the thermal signature of entrained fluid. Its trapped experiment is background,
  not the boundary condition used in this free-sphere prototype.
- Fouxon et al., *Lamb-type solution and properties of unsteady Stokes equations*,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00387, gives the exterior pressure/Helmholtz-mode
  construction and its oscillating-sphere specialization.
