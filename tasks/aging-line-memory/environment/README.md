# Two residence recorders in a glass

A fluorescent particle occupies one basin at a time in a specimen with N ordinary basins and two labeled reporter basins, A and B. The ordinary binding depths E_i are independent exponential variables of mean T_g in [0.9,1.1]. Each ordinary basin has its own distinct line. The same specimen keeps its depths for the entire observation. A fresh-basin calibration measures the probability that E_i exceeds the input `threshold` in [0.2,2], rounded to eight decimal places.

The depths of the two reporters are T_g ln N + δ_A and T_g ln N + δ_B. The input `offsets=[δ_A,δ_B]` gives both detunings in energy units, from −0.72 to 0.35. At time zero put the particle in A. At constant `temperature` T in [0.53,0.71], the particle leaves a basin of depth E at rate exp(−E/T). Desorption produces a short flight that ends in one of the N+2 basins with uniform probability, independently on each flight. Flight time is negligible. The new destination can be a previously visited basin, including the one just left. The microscopic attempt time and Boltzmann constant are one.

An independent Poisson shutter takes one exposure at its first click and ends the launch. Its rate is s/N^(T_g/T) in microscopic time, with `clock`=s in [0.55,1.45]. Read the line occupied at that instant. Two separate clocks measure the **total** time spent in A and in B up to the click, including all returns. Divide their readings by N^(T_g/T). The shutter keeps running while the particle is elsewhere. The residence clocks and the shutter do not alter any escape rate.

Select the specified residence readings `levels=[x,y]`. The A reading x is in [0.25,1.65]. The B reading y is either zero or in [0.18,1.10]. For a positive reading select a one-sided bin from that value to that value plus ε. A zero B reading means that the particle never occupied B, so select exactly zero. The experiment uses one launch on each newly drawn specimen. Pool selected shots across specimens, take N to infinity, then let ε tend to zero. Report the indicated conditional probability.

* For `read="A"` or `read="B"`, count a success when the terminal line is the indicated reporter.
* For `read="host"`, count a terminal ordinary line. If a `band=[lo,hi]` is supplied, also require lo≤N exp(−E_i/T_g)≤hi for that final ordinary basin. The bounds are in [0.10,3] with width at least 0.35. The band selects successes only. The denominator counts all shots with the requested residence readings.

For a check on a glass with T_g=1 take T=0.6, s=1, offsets=[0,0] and levels=[0.8,0]. The terminal B probability is zero. The A probability is 0.68095082. With levels=[0.8,0.55] the A and B probabilities are 0.12559963 and 0.57100954. The ordinary probability for band=[0.25,1.65] then is 0.10029672. At a positive B clock both reporters have been visited, and a particle can have moved between them more than once.

Implement `Model` in /app/model.py. Its `fit(records)` receives input/value dictionaries, sets `depth_scale` to the fitted T_g, and returns self. Its `predict(inputs)` returns a flat finite real NumPy array in input order, including an empty array for an empty list. The calibration tolerance is 0.00001. Each new reading has absolute tolerance 0.004. The starter uses equilibrium weights for the reporters known to have been visited and for an average ordinary medium.
