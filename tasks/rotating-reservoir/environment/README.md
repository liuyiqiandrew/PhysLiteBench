# Calorimetry with a rotating reservoir

A particle of mass one moves on an unbounded two-dimensional plane in a fixed laboratory harmonic potential U=(stiffness_x*x²+stiffness_y*y²)/2. Boltzmann's constant is one. Position is r=(x,y), velocity is v, and R=[[0,-1],[1,0]] denotes a counterclockwise quarter turn.

Two independent ideal classical Markov reservoirs act on the particle. Reservoir A is stationary at temperature temperature_a. Its deterministic force is -drag*v, with the same unknown positive drag in every experiment. Reservoir B has the prescribed rigid mean motion u(r)=angular_speed*R*r, temperature temperature_b and known drag coefficient b=0.9. Its deterministic force is -b*(v-u). Each reservoir supplies its own independent, isotropic Gaussian white thermal force, with its equilibrium Langevin strength at that reservoir's stated temperature and drag. There are no other forces or noises. These force laws are the complete laboratory model; the stated trap includes all static mechanical forces.

An ideal motor maintains reservoir B's angular speed. A thermostat maintains temperature_b. The calorimeter measures the long-time mean heat removed by that thermostat from the internal thermal degrees of freedom of reservoir B, positive for heat entering B from the particle. The empty-apparatus background is subtracted. Reservoir A has a separate thermostat. The reservoir fields and temperatures are unaffected by the particle. After all controls are set, the apparatus reaches its unique stationary state before recording a signal. Finite particle inertia is retained.

Each experiment is a dictionary containing angular_speed in [-.65,.65], stiffness_x in [.8,1.8], stiffness_y in [2,3.2], temperature_a in [.6,1.6] and temperature_b in [.6,1.4]. All controls are exact. The only unknown is drag in [.4,1.1]. All allowed controls give stable stationary dynamics. Calibration records in data/calibration.json contain input, measured value and independent Gaussian standard uncertainty sigma=0.001. Calibration is performed with angular_speed=0, using varied trap stiffnesses and reservoir temperatures.

Implement Model.fit(records), returning self and storing the fitted drag. Model.predict(experiments) must return a finite NumPy array with shape (len(experiments),), in input order, containing the specified calorimeter heat rates. Any implementation satisfying this interface may be used.
