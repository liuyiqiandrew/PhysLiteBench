# Hydrodynamic heating, revision 1

This task separates an exact nonlocal optical boundary solution from a local-intensity heating assumption. Both controls include all four transverse/longitudinal slab modes, the full frequency-dependent Drude response, internal reflections, tangential Maxwell matching and hard-wall zero normal current. No optical mode or known input is omitted. The physical error is the conversion of those correct fields into regional irreversible heating.

The public apparatus specifies the ideal compressible electron-fluid equations, local momentum friction and conservative pressure. The plasma definition omega_p²=n0*q²/m_e and J=q*n0*v determine the collision loss from ordinary mechanical work. For complex amplitudes with exp(-i*omega*t), the period-averaged heat density delivered to the stationary bath is

`q_drag = gamma*|J|²/(2*omega_p²)`.

Multiplying the real current equation by J/omega_p² and using charge continuity gives electron energy density `(J²+beta²*rho²)/(2*omega_p²)` and pressure-energy flux `beta²*rho*J/omega_p²`. Its period-averaged normal flux is `beta²*Re(rho*J_z*)/(2*omega_p²)`. The regional heat equals the decrease of electromagnetic plus pressure-energy flux across the detector window. Pressure stores and transports energy; only the specified drag irreversibly heats the bath.

The completed source instead evaluates the local Drude loss `omega*Im(epsilon_T)*|E|²/2`, using the exact nonlocal electric field. At normal incidence only transverse modes are excited, J=sigma_T E throughout the slab, so this expression exactly equals collision heating for every depth window and every allowed plasma frequency. All calibration settings therefore agree exactly. At oblique incidence the longitudinal component obeys J_L=i*omega*E_L, while the transverse current remains sigma_T*E_T. Their superposition no longer supports the local-intensity rule, even though the field solution is correct.

The oracle integrates collision loss using the complete mode amplitudes. The independent verifier derives a first-order spatial system for `(E_x,H_y,J_z,rho)` directly from Maxwell, continuity and fluid momentum equations, solves its two-point boundary problem with a matrix exponential, and evaluates the total energy flux at the window endpoints. It does not contain the oracle's drag-heating integral or four-wave basis.

The ideal hydrodynamic response and hard-wall boundary are related to [Hiremath, Zschiedrich and Schmidt, JCP 231 (2012)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1201.1527). Planar nonlocal wave propagation is discussed by [David, Mortensen and Christensen, Scientific Reports 3,2526 (2013)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3755289/). These are background sources; the task defines its own ideal continuum material with beta=.1c, fixed gamma=.06 and no additional quantum, diffusion, viscosity or surface-loss terms. It does not claim those ideal constants describe a particular experimental metal.
