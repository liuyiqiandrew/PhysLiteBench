# Avalanche charge spectra

A hard-dead-space multiplication layer and a uniform weighting field produce a mixed distribution of the analog induced charge, even after both terminal times and carrier packet heights are specified. The coupling comes from a shared field during multiplication and from observing two induced currents on the same set of ballistic tracks. The public transport evaluates the terminal clock coincidence and integrates the hazard across every eligible segment. The new observable is an ideal analog charge measurement after this coincidence.

The positive solution clips the domain of free impact times for each labeled genealogy. Within the cells cut by births and collections at gate endpoints, induced charge is affine. A constant cell at the requested line contributes its full area. For the continuum, each intersected level set contributes its length divided by the magnitude of the charge gradient, or the inverse absolute slope in one dimension. Applying the same weights to the second charge produces its conditional expectation.

The independent private solver propagates the birth, reset and contact times and integrates a physical interval of measured charge between two thresholds in chronological time strips. It uses three successively narrower charge intervals to estimate either the zero-width mass or the derivative of interval mass with respect to width. The analytic worked example checks both intensities and both conditional waveforms independently.

The public starter has fixed Gaussian electronic resolution. A stronger negative control calculates the ideal charge spectrum but triggers an independent avalanche for the second read gate under the same terminal times and heights. It passes both intensity groups and misses the conditional current. Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/validate_avalanche_packet_current.py` to compare the two methods, all controls, the public example and the frozen data.
