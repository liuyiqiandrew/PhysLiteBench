# Coincident induced-charge spectra

The two ideal gate integrators are connected to one avalanche. A single free impact can vary both gate charges together, giving a diagonal ridge in the two-charge plane even when both one-channel spectra are locally continuous. Histories with two free impacts can populate an area, while a fixed count of pairs crossing each gate gives point masses.

The positive solution divides the feasible chronological impact times at all relevant gate and collection boundaries. On each resulting cell the charges depend affinely on the free times. A rank-zero map sends an entire cell to a point. At rank one, the probability on the preimage is divided by the nonzero singular value to give density per Euclidean arclength. At rank two the absolute determinant converts time area into charge area. The same history weights give the third-gate conditional mean.

The independent verifier integrates finite rectangular apertures directly by placing their four charge bounds into the chronologically propagated time strips. It extrapolates two sizes to zero. Before collection the derivative of either gate in one birth time is either zero or −(vₑ+vₕ), so a regular ridge is horizontal, vertical or diagonal. Two further rectangular aspect ratios determine which projected width converts to circular-aperture arclength. Four closed-form examples check the line, area and point normalizations.

The independent-channel starter combines calibrated single-integrator spectra from separate avalanches. The separate-read negative control keeps the exact joint two-channel intensities but starts a fresh avalanche for the read gate. All 24 held-out targets differ from the examples. Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/validate_avalanche_packet_current.py` to check the frozen values, both independent solvers, the examples and the controls.
