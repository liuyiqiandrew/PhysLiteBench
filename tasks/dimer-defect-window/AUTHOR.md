# Dimer defect window, revision 2

The optical marks probe the topology of the transition contours of two independent equilibrium coverings. The first revision concerned a single punctured triangular sheet. Luna solved that revision. The second uses two open rectangular sheets with only horizontal and vertical binding. Separate loops at the two marks give a different signal from one common loop.

On the reference square the odds for a vertical bond are (z ratio)², so every calibration row identifies z. The 0.004 allowance is four parts per thousand of event probability. Seed 81913 sets the held-out preparations. The completed isolated-plaquette approximation retains the calibration and the probability of a local four-edge resonance. It cannot generate a single contour enclosing two distinct plaquettes.

## Counting common loops

Let G(s,t) be the mean of s to the number of contours surrounding exactly one mark, times t to the number surrounding both. A contour surrounding neither contributes one. With allow_separate=true the readout is the coefficient of t to the requested count in G(1,t). The veto instead uses G(0,t), with zero to the zeroth power equal to one. Every common contour crosses a path from either mark to the nearest outer edge. These crossings bound the polynomial degree by the smaller of the two marks' distances to the exterior dual face.

Use a black-to-white bipartite Kasteleyn matrix K on the square grid. Let A and B in SL(2,C) be the transports across two disjoint slits from the marks to the outer face. For a bond crossed in the opposite direction insert the inverse transport. Replace each scalar entry of K by its scalar times the appropriate two-dimensional transport. The determinant of this block matrix divided by det(K)² equals the expectation of the product of half traces around transition loops. In the expansion each nontrivial contour has two alternating assignments to the sheets, supplying the factor of two in the ordinary partition function. A doubled bond has unit return. The traces of A, B and AB are 2s, 2s and 2t. On this disk with two marks each simple contour enclosing both has the trace of AB, independently of its starting point or orientation. [Kenyon, double-dimer loop weights](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4158)

The oracle sends its two slits upwards, uses real alternating vertical Kasteleyn signs and extracts coefficients around the unit circle. The private reference sends one slit left and the other right, uses imaginary vertical weights and a different pair of transport matrices. A finite-rank determinant lemma reduces the calculation to the cut bonds. Interpolation on real Chebyshev nodes extracts the probabilities. A positive enumeration of pairs of matchings traverses and classifies their actual contour components on small sheets. The validator also checks normalization and the veto inequality on new preparations. Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/validate_dimer_defect_window.py` to reproduce the data and controls.
