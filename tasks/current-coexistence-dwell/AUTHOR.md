# Author analysis

This original rare-current task is inspired by thermodynamic conditioning and Poisson processes in the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

For a path with fraction (f\) spent in B, tilt the counter by (q=\log(\mathrm{dd}/\mathrm{du})\). The channel cumulant functions (\lambda_j(q)=g[u_j(e^q-1)+d_j(e^{-q}-1)]\) agree at q. The path acquires the same exponential change-of-measure factor whatever f. Under this tilt its counter mean is (N[j_A+f(j_B-j_A)]\) with strictly positive variance proportional to N. The lattice local central limit theorem holds uniformly in f because both tilted count intensities are bounded and Poisson differences have span one.

At an interior target fraction b the conditioning selects (f=b+O(N^{-1/2})\). The common counter factor times (1/[N(j_B-j_A)]\) multiplies the continuous dwell density at b. The two zero-switch occupation atoms are exponentially suppressed. At b=0 or b=1 the corresponding atom instead dominates because its count probability is order (N^{-1/2}\) under the common tilt, while nearby continuous paths give order (N^{-1}\). The initial probabilities ensure both atoms are present.

For a path with n≥1 switches and initial channel j, write (m_i\) for the number of switches leaving i and (k_i\) for the number of dwell intervals in it. At fixed fractions (f_A=1-b,f_B=b\), the path density is proportional to (p_j\prod_i(\gamma_i^{m_i} f_i^{k_i-1}/(k_i-1)!)\) where (γ_i=g\,\mathrm{switch\_rates}_i\). The survival factor (e^{-\gamma_A f_A-\gamma_B f_B}\) is common. At fixed occupancy the lengths in each channel are uniform on the corresponding simplex. If there are at least two pieces in the initial channel, the first piece divided by that channel's fraction has a Beta(1,k_j−1) law. If there is one piece, the scaled time converges to 1. At a window endpoint exactly equal to 1, the finite-count occupancy layer approaches its two sides symmetrically and contributes half. Integrating over the leading even Gaussian in f gives that factor independent of the switching density, which is continuous there.

The oracle sums the switched path densities. The reference uses Bessel resummation for the full dwell density and integrates the exact finite-size tilted Skellam law to check the interior boundary and the endpoint atom. The shortcut conditions only the integer switch record and the time gate against an ordinary two-channel Markov process, without using the counter to fix occupation fractions.
