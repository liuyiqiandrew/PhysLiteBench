# Stellar tracer mixing, revision 4

Two labelled stellar populations source a common gravitational acceleration on a periodic line. A first kick leaves fine structure in velocity after its spatial density has phase mixed. A second kick can turn that structure back into a measurable density pulse. The delay, population labels and spectrometer band distinguish gravitationally amplified echoes from free streaming.

Revisions 1–3 used bound isochrone orbits. Their sources and scores remain in Git and the qualification report. This revision replaces that apparatus with a plane-periodic stream.

## Derivation

Write a=k₁, b=k₂ and k=b−a. For population s let f_s(v) denote the initial velocity distribution and φ_s(z)=∫dv f_s(v) exp(−izv). At a spatial harmonic q a unit cosine velocity kick with population coefficients d_s generates a freely advected linear perturbation proportional to −d_s f′_s. The acceleration generated by the density is iμρ_q/q, where μ is strength. The retarded integral equation for this common acceleration has kernel μ t Σ_s φ_s(qt), t≥0. Its L¹ norm is at most μ Σ_s ρ_s/(q σ_s)² < 0.68 for every active harmonic. Thus the prepared waves are stable and their linear transients decay.

Transform the retarded response at frequency qv, leaving the cosine factor 1/2 outside the formula. Define

    H_s(q,v) = μ ∫₀∞ dt t φ_s(qt) exp(iqvt)
    A_s(q,v;d) = d_s + Σ_j H_j(q,v) d_j / [1−Σ_j H_j(q,v)].

Long after kick 1, the −a harmonic retains its ballistic factor exp(iavt) and coefficient −f′_s A_s(−a,v;first)/2. Acting on this filament with the second impulse and its wake gives a term proportional to T from its velocity derivative. Free propagation at the mixed harmonic cancels the large phase at t=bT/k+delay. Its bare source spectrum (after dividing by T) is

    F_s(v) = (ia/4) f′_s(v) A_s(−a,v;first) A_s(b,v;second).

The mixed wave itself gravitates. A band-limited detector responds to its acceleration with susceptibility

    H_W(q,v) = (−iμ/q) ∫₀∞ dt exp(iqvt) ∫du W(u) f′_s(u) exp(−iqut).

For a receiver on population s the integrand is W F_s + H_W(k,v) Σ_j F_j / [1−Σ_j H_j(k,v)]. Multiply by exp[−i(k delay v + angle)], integrate over v, and take the real part. A flat band recovers H_s, but filtering the source in the common denominator would discard gravitating stars.

The oracle computes the positive-time Gaussian transforms using the complex error function. The private reference instead propagates the causal linear impulse responses on a real retarded-time grid. It convolves each population's two acceleration histories with the Fourier transform of its velocity gradient, then evolves the mixed density and the filtered response in time. Halving the grid spacing from 0.025 to 0.0125 changes the private signals by at most 0.000009. The oracle and frozen reference differ by less than 0.000004. The allowed absolute signal error is 0.00045.

A peer independently evolved the three finite-delay Vlasov harmonics in position/velocity Fourier space with a transport/force split, 1,024 velocity points and a time step of 0.04. Two off-grid settings with T=16 and T=32, extrapolated in 1/T, gave 0.000751188 and 0.00370657. The differences from the limiting oracle were 0.000000019 and 0.0000011. The second setting measures stars that did not receive the second imposed kick.

With self-gravity off, the first calibration row has an amplitude proportional to exp[−(0.75σ)²/2], strictly monotone on the allowed range. The free-streaming shortcut and oracle both recover σ=0.947. The shortcut's largest absolute errors on joint density, cross-population transfer and spectral bands are 0.00722, 0.00636 and 0.00684. The gravitationally mediated readout can remain finite when no single population receives both kicks.
