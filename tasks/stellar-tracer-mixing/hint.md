In the slow preparation, label orbits by angular momentum and radial action.
Across an instantaneous switch compare the two energies at the same radius
and velocity. A phase-mixed orbital shell samples radius in proportion to
the time spent there. Apply this averaging after each switch.
For many weak cycles use the radial action as the transport coordinate at fixed angular momentum. Recover both first and second action increments or use invariance of canonical phase volume to relate the drift to the variance. Implement removal at the upper endpoint in this coordinate.
For a fixed hold the two energy changes act on the same orbit, at phases separated by a fixed physical time. The leading action variance can vanish on particular orbits. Examine what that implies for flux through those actions.
