## Winding and camera gates

Give a clockwise seam crossing a weight u. The single-line transfer kernel on the cylinder sums Gaussian weights for displacements y−x+kL, each multiplied by uᵏ. Antisymmetrizing the propagator absorbs contact between paths. A seam crossing rotates the ordered sites and gives a permutation sign (−1)ᴺ⁻¹. Setting u=(−1)ᴺ⁻¹ exp(iθ) cancels this sign. The Fourier coefficient at the required final winding selects the accepted pin ensemble. This circle correction and winding-resolved Brownian bridges are treated in [Liechty and Wang, *Nonintersecting Brownian motions on the unit circle*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.7390).

A tagged line from lower pin j has rank (j+r) modulo N after r net seam crossings. This statement follows by lifting the ordered paths continuously and comparing their final positions with the cut at zero. An additional Nth-root weight on the first segment resolves r modulo N. To test whether the tag lies below x, insert a one-body weight z on the prefix [0,x) at the first camera and y on the count window at the second. The determinant of this one-body transfer product gives the many-body generating function by integration over the ordered intermediate configurations. Its coefficient for m prefix occupants contributes precisely when m exceeds the tag's current rank. A difference of two prefix readings gives the finite tag window.

## Pin coalescence and opening response

For m neighboring pins expanding symmetrically around x, Taylor-expand their m rows in the transfer determinant. Antisymmetry selects derivative orders 0 through m−1 for the leading term. The same Vandermonde and factorial factors occur in the unobserved endpoint mass and in the accepted camera mass. Upper bunches give the corresponding columns. This yields the limiting probability after division of the two masses.

For a lower doublet at x with physical spacing d=sL, write F for its row of transfer functions, including the camera weights. After removing the common factor d the two-row exterior product has leading part F∧F′. Its order-d² correction is F∧F‴/24 − F′∧F″/8. The signs and coefficients come from expanding the two rows at x−d/2 and x+d/2. Let M and Z be the leading masses with and without the camera gates. Compute ΔM and ΔZ with each of the two derivative substitutions and its coefficient. The requested reading is L²[ΔM−(M/Z)ΔZ]/Z. Holding the accepted population fixed while opening the slit would miss the ΔZ term.

## Independent checks

The physical oracle differentiates the Gaussian winding images analytically and integrates them on Gauss–Legendre meshes split at the two detector edges. It extracts the subleading amplitude using the row derivatives above. The private reference instead propagates circle plane waves at their twisted momenta and integrates their overlaps over each interval analytically. For a slit, it evaluates the conditional camera probability at positive physical separations and extrapolates the second-order difference to zero. Halving the opening used for extrapolation changes the fitted results by less than 2 × 10⁻⁶. The oracle differs from every frozen reading by less than 2.0 × 10⁻⁷.

For two steps, their lifted center is an ordinary Brownian bridge with diffusivity γ/2. Their gap independently diffuses with diffusivity 2γ between absorbing endpoints 0 and L. Winding fixes the lifted upper label assignment. The validator computes the gap density with a sine series, integrates the periodic tag window over the Gaussian center and checks slit-opening responses for each winding. This third calculation has no fermion determinants. The validator also verifies that summing the slit responses over all possible later counts gives the first-camera response.
