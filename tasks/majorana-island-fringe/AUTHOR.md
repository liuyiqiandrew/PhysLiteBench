# Majorana island fringe, revision 5

Parity-preserving gates on a charge-isolated superconducting island rotate its Majorana modes. A probe in coherent superposition between two pulse sequences measures their many-body relative phase. A second qubit controls the coupling during the pulses and selects which trials the detector keeps. The physical quantities here are the parity-constrained thermal average, the spinor lift of each rotation and the postselected two-arm coherence. A relative 2π pulse reverses the fringe even though the Majorana coordinates return to their original values. Joint parity measurements on superconducting islands and trace identities for paired fermions motivate the model. [Hyart et al., 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1303.4379) · [Klich, 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7824)

## Original island and qubit measurement

For arm a let Uₐ₊ and Uₐ₋ be the ordered island unitaries at the two meter Z values. On meter outcome s the island operator for this arm is

    Kₐ = (Uₐ₊ + s exp(−iφ) Uₐ₋)/2.

The unnormalized probability of retaining the shot is p = (⟨K₀†K₀⟩+⟨K₁†K₁⟩)/2. The measured reading is V Re[exp(−iλ)⟨K₀†K₁⟩]/p. These expressions follow by projecting the complete two-qubit state once at the end. They include interference between the meter histories as well as between the arm histories. The identical-arm calibration gives V cos λ for every retained outcome.

In the full Gibbs state the mode pairs are independent with polarizations tⱼ=tanh(βEⱼ/2). The contractions within a pair are ⟨γ₂ⱼγ₂ⱼ₊₁⟩=itⱼ. For total parity P and required parity η, each charge-projected average is

    ⟨O⟩η = [⟨O⟩ + η⟨OP⟩]/[1+η∏tⱼ].

The oracle computes each ordered pulse product directly. For a rotation α, its two terms are c=cos(α/2) times the identity and s=−sin(α/2) times γ(u)γ(v). In the Wick matrix take the ordered forms sγ(u), γ(v) for every pulse, put their pair contractions above the diagonal and antisymmetrize. Add c to the upper-diagonal element of each pulse pair and −c to its transpose. In the Pfaffian expansion, selecting this c removes the adjacent two forms without changing the sign of the remaining pairing. All other terms contract the selected bilinears by Wick's theorem. This identity yields the signed ordered expectation even when a trace crosses zero. For ⟨OP⟩ append the n parity bilinears, each with c=0 and s=−i.

## Independent checks

The private reference multiplies Majorana monomials explicitly in the addressed contact basis and uses anticommutation to track the sign. It computes the charge insertion by replacing each tⱼ by 1/tⱼ in the contractions and multiplying by ∏tⱼ. This follows by taking the trace of Pρ separately on each thermal pair. A third implementation evolves the actual occupation-basis density amplitudes with Pauli-string Majoranas and projects the meter. The validator compares this calculation against the sparse reference for every fixture through six fermion modes. Larger preparations include both contact frames and noncommuting pulses on islands through 32 fermion modes.

Seed 718305 gives 12 calibration records and four families of eight held-out measurements. The winding family rotates one arm through 2π before a further pulse. The meter family changes coherent coupling to the same retained meter. The charge family probes a cold odd island where discarding the conserved parity changes the occupations appreciably. The extended family uses delocalized contacts on larger islands. The fixed prediction tolerance of 0.003 resolves the observable to three parts per thousand of full contrast. Independent calculations agree below 2 × 10⁻⁹. The visibility is identifiable from a linear fit to the calibration signals, whose largest rounding error is 5 × 10⁻⁹.

The revision 1 completed shortcut evolves both qubits coherently but uses an island in thermal contact with a parity reservoir. It fits the visibility and misses the cold fixed-charge family by up to 0.594. The revision 1 public starter instead uses the correctly charge-conditioned mean pair parity as a static classical field. It calibrates correctly and misses the coherent meter family by up to 1.415. Both models give finite predictions and leave different signatures in the verifier metrics.

## Revision 2, emitted microwave mode

The island now displaces a resonator by ±α depending on the local pair parity. Between displacements, a passive beam splitter sends part of the resonator to an outgoing vacuum mode. The homodyne receiver retains a voltage bin in a specified coherent superposition of the outgoing pulses. Longitudinal Majorana-parity readout by a microwave resonator and mode-selective optical homodyne measurements are described by [Grimsmo and Smith, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.99.235420) and [Raymer et al., 1995](https://doi.org/10.1364/JOSAB.12.001801).

For each retained spin history the force factors into the pair-parity projector (1+σQ)/2 and displacement D(σα). Carry the complex coherent amplitudes of the cavity and output modes through their beam splitters. Applying D(α) after an accumulated cavity displacement d multiplies a path by exp[i Im(d*α)]. The remaining accumulated displacement at the output is a vector. For two paths let their final vectors be A and B, δ=B−A, and v the passive image of a unit initial cavity amplitude. The undriven multimode optical covariance in displacement units is M=I+2 n̄ vv†. Let g be the normalized selected mode times exp(iθ), with zero component on the surviving cavity.

The optical trace with no voltage restriction is W=exp[i Im(A†B)−δ†Mδ/2]. Inserting exp(isX) yields W exp[is μ−s²σ²/2] by the Weyl product law, with

    μ = Re[g†(A+B)]/√2 + i Im(g†Mδ)/√2,     σ² = g†Mg/2.

The voltage bin therefore multiplies W by Φ((hi−μ)/σ)−Φ((lo−μ)/σ). The imaginary part of the displaced cross history's mean carries the phase of the voltage-dependent spin coherence. A real diagonal acceptance probability misses it. Weighted spin traces for both the cross signal and the individual arm acceptances give the normalized conditional measurement.

The independent cavity reference constructs an explicit spin density matrix on the addressed Clifford algebra, represents the initial thermal resonator by a Gaussian mixture of coherent states, applies each force and beam splitter to their amplitudes, and performs two numerical quadratures. Gauss–Hermite integrates the initial thermal displacement. Gauss–Legendre integrates the complex conjugate wavefunction product over the voltage bin. Coherent-state inner products trace out the surviving cavity and unobserved output modes. The two algorithms agree within 2 × 10⁻⁹ on the twelve added cases. The completed shortcut retains the spin–resonator interaction and the qubit meter selection but assumes retaining the homodyne bin is independent of the arm measurement. The public starter supplies the coherent island-and-qubit solution for all protocols without the resonator.

## Revision 3, incident pair source

The two emission alternatives differ in temporal and spatial mode shape. They may carry partly distinguishable marker states. A passive network mixes the feed into the outgoing receiver and surviving cavity while a second line bypasses them. The retained arm signal probes boson exchange and how strongly the source records which alternative occurred. The new starter reproduces all 44 earlier held-out readings and replaces the incoming pair by vacuum. It misses the two new groups by 0.246 and 0.105. A separate completed shortcut propagates both injected two-photon states exactly but assigns orthogonal marker states to the alternatives. Its observed errors are 0.0235 and 0.0321. Source emission is conditioned once before the arm experiment. Its probability does not vary with the spin history.

Represent a two-photon state by the complex symmetric matrix S in |S⟩=∑ Sᵢⱼ aᵢ†aⱼ†|vac⟩/√2. The normalized packet pair has S=(fgᵀ+gfᵀ)/√[2(1+|f†g|²)]. The overlap between two states is the Frobenius inner product Tr(Sᵢ†Sⱼ). Sum all bra and ket source choices with coefficients cᵢ* cⱼ ⟨dᵢ|dⱼ⟩ and divide by their contracted source norm. Here c₀=1. The passive network sends a packet vector through the same beam splitters as an input coherent amplitude.

For a displacement k the matrix element ⟨Sᵢ|D(k)|Sⱼ⟩ divided by the vacuum overlap is Tr(Sᵢ†Sⱼ)−2uᵢ†uⱼ+bᵢ* bⱼ/2, where uᵢ=Sᵢk* and bᵢ=k†uᵢ. This follows by normal ordering the displacement. At most two photons can be removed from either state. Inserting exp(itX) in a spin cross history sets k=δ+it g/√2 in this polynomial and gives a degree-four polynomial in real t multiplying the Gaussian characteristic function from revision 2. The inverse Fourier transform of its t^j term is i^j times the j-th derivative of the complex-shifted Gaussian density. Integration over the specified voltage window gives the CDF for j=0 and differences of Gaussian derivatives at the two endpoints for j≥1.

The independent reference propagates each incoming packet and resolves it into the observed temporal mode and the orthogonal complement. The measured mode has zero, one or two photons with corresponding displaced Hermite wavefunctions. Residual photon creators and annihilators are contracted explicitly with the residual displacement. Direct thermal and voltage quadratures are performed with spin density matrices on the addressed Clifford algebra. This retains both source alternatives and the bypass line. All 12 new oracle readings agree with this independent reference to 2.0×10⁻⁹, including rounding. The validator also checks full bypass of both photons, orthogonal source markers and the single-bin example.

## Revision 4, dispersive echo

In the new mode each local parity rotates the current resonator phase immediately before its outcoupling. All longitudinal kicks vanish. Thermal light and heralded number states have zero mean field, but even a phase rotation of π on one arm changes the relative sign of odd occupation components. Because the full optical preparation commutes with total photon number, the cross operator reduced to the observed single mode commutes with its number. The homodyne acceptance on k photons is the integral of the squared k-th harmonic-oscillator wavefunction over the voltage window. The local-oscillator phase drops out of these runs, whereas relative phases within the chosen temporal receiver mode remain observable.

For two spin histories let u and v be the distinct images of the incoming thermal cavity mode. To generate the detected occupation insert R(z)=I+(z−1)gg†, where g is the selected receiver mode. Write p=n̄/(1+n̄), a=u†Rv. The thermal sum over incoming number multiplies the generating function by (1−p)/(1−pa). With an injected pair, assemble the remaining contractions among the two left packet vectors f and the two right packet vectors h. Each contraction is f†Rh+p(f†Rv)(u†Rh)/(1−pa). The two bosons produce the permanent of this 2×2 matrix, divided by each pair’s incoming normalization. These expressions follow by summing the permanent of n repeated copies of each thermal vector and two additional packets against the geometric number law. Source amplitudes and markers weight the different pair alternatives as in revision 3. A 48-point Fourier projection resolves the measured occupation. Its tail is negligible with n̄≤0.65 and only two added photons.

The private calculation instead integrates coherent initial oscillator amplitudes over the thermal distribution. Its path-dependent modes are propagated directly through the numbered beam splitters. Residual photon states are traced explicitly and displaced Hermite wavefunctions are integrated over voltage. The two new six-reading groups agree with the characteristic-function oracle to 1.2×10⁻⁹. The largest discrepancies of the zero-shift starter are 0.304 and 0.462, while an alternative model that records each local parity in a perfect register misses by 0.283 and 0.263. Both controls reproduce the 56 previous readings.

In the public one-release example the diagonal voltage distributions have variance 3/4. The off-diagonal thermal component has unconditioned trace 1/2, and its normalized signed voltage density has variance 3/8. The ratio of the two Gaussian window integrals, multiplied by 1/2, gives the displayed fringe.

## Revision 5, two normal charge dots

A separate readout uses two spin-polarized dot orbitals. One electron is prepared coherently across them. Each tunnel contact transfers a fermion between one dot and an island Dirac channel, followed by a dwell that acts only if both dots are occupied. The charging phase is a two-body interaction. The exit analyzer recombines the two single-electron dot states and cannot resolve which dot was occupied. A control that records which dot was occupied loses the interference term in that projection. Prior readouts and calibration retain their original protocols.

The oracle restricts the prepared island to the at most six addressed orthogonal contact Majoranas, completing an odd set with one unused contact. It reconstructs each thermal Gaussian density from its two-point skew matrix K. Positive eigenvectors of iK determine canonical Majorana pairs, and each pair of polarization t contributes (1+tQ)/2. The fixed initial charge is incorporated by summing ordinary and total-parity-inserted thermal moments with the appropriate relative weight. The dots precede the island in the tensor order, so every odd island operator carries the dot-parity Jordan–Wigner string. Both rank-two tunneling channels and the diagonal dot charging dwell are then propagated as finite-dimensional operators. A unit-strength tunneling operator H₀ has eigenvalues 0,±1. Its evolution is I−i sin(τ) H₀+[cos(τ)−1] H₀².

The independent computation expands each gate and the final dot projector in the Clifford algebra of the distinct contacted island Majoranas and four dot Majoranas. Multiplication tracks the parity of the permutation needed to sort the operator strings. Each branch product is contracted against the initial island moments from fermionic Wick recursion and the independently evaluated two-dot matrix elements. It never reconstructs the reduced density matrix or exponentiates matrices. The analyzer projector equals n_u−n₀n₁ for the normalized analyzed orbital u. The meter’s alternatives and the arm numerator and retained probability use the same dot selection.

In the public swap-and-return example let p₀ be the probability that the island mode is empty. Its fixed-even-sector polarization is (t+t²)/(1+t³), t=tanh(0.75), so p₀ is one half plus one half of this polarization. On an empty channel the two retained arms coincide. On a filled one the second arm is removed by the coherent dot analyzer. The acceptance and normalized signal are therefore (1+p₀)/2 and 2p₀/(1+p₀).
