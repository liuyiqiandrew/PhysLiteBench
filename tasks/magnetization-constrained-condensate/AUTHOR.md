# Magnetization-constrained condensate

The starting themes are quantum occupation counting and pulse-controlled spin dynamics in Princeton's graduate physics preliminary exams. The ideal spin-one gas with a quadratic level splitting and fixed magnetization has distinct saturation regimes. This task's original observable is a signed, same-shot readout after a coherent spin mixing pulse. [Princeton exam archive](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams) [Ideal spin-one saturation with fixed magnetization](https://arxiv.org/abs/1505.03999)

The excited-state density of spin j at dimensionless gap d_j is (mass*T/(2*pi))^(3/2)*Li_(3/2)(exp(-d_j)). A saturated component has d_j=0. The oracle enumerates possible faces of the two-chemical-potential feasibility region. On a one-component face, the thermal magnetization relative to that component is fixed by the exact two totals and fixes the remaining chemical potential. At a two-component vertex the two condensate populations follow from the residual density and magnetization. When all three gaps vanish, the residuals restrict the macroscopic Fock occupations to a segment with one state per permissible lattice point. Its thermodynamic measure is uniform along the segment before the weak split tilts the weights. An interior thermodynamic state has no zero-momentum density.

The phase between the two macroscopic occupations is uniform in the limiting shot distribution, obtained from fixed-number spin states. Conditional on their densities, the measured Y is a constant plus a cosine of that phase, with amplitude proportional to the geometric mean of the two densities. The oracle integrates its arcsine window probability across the tilted segment. The approximation fits the spin-balanced calibration by counting the three critical thermal densities, then uses a deterministic condensate and propagates only the averaged spin transfer. That treatment loses the exact magnetization constraint on the thermal cloud and the bunching of the measured spin projection.

The independent reference computes thermal densities and pressure by momentum quadrature, determines the saturation face through two-dimensional constrained pressure minimization, and evaluates window probabilities using an independently parameterized spin fraction integral. It checks the projection window against finite Fock-state number statistics from direct exponentiation of the exact spin-J pulse.
