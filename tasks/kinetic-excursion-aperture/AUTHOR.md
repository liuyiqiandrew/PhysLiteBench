# Reference model

The kinetic propagator in the half-line starts at the wall with positive speed u. The probability density of the first contact at time t and inward speed a is a times the free endpoint density at (0,−a), times erf(√(3ua/(Dt))). Subtracting paths after their first contact from the free propagator gives the killed propagator. The solution integrates this renewal identity on each arm of the excursion. Velocity reverses under time reversal. A kick δ at the aperture changes the initial velocity of the reversed return arm to v at the wall and its final velocity to −w−δ at x, for pre-kick velocity w. The inward flux factor v cancels in each first-return ratio. The free aperture conditioning and the velocity variance 2Dt are given in the public code.

The fixture generator takes a different route. It samples the latent velocity at the observed position by Gaussian state-space conditioning, then bisects each arm using the exact joint Gaussian bridge law for position and velocity. An arrival contributes only if every intermediate measured position is positive. Each shot uses four independent streams of 131072 bridges per conditional probability, with 127 interior checks per arm. On the 21 shots the largest discrepancy against the independently integrated killed propagator is 0.00108. The camera tolerance is 0.006. Running with 40 and 80 quadrature nodes per integral changes every reading by less than 0.00015. The equal-time, equal-speed direction reading is one half at a zero cut by time reversal. The zero-kick posterior is one half by exchange of the independent coin labels.

The public Gaussian approximation and the outbound-only killed approximation both fit calibration. The latter ignores premature touches on the return arm and misses a camera reading by 0.1888 in clearance, 0.0659 in direction and 0.0623 in pulse fraction. Run `python scripts/validate_kinetic_excursion_aperture.py` at repository root to check these diagnostics. Pass `--generate` to recompute the private measurements.
