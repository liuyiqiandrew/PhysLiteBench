# Author analysis

This original problem is inspired by Gaussian energy fluctuation and equilibrium ensemble reasoning in the [Princeton graduate physics preliminary exam archive](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

The annealed critical densities are products of shifted Gaussians. Their physically feasible ordinary cone has x_0≥0, x_0+x_1≥0 and x_0+x_1+x_2≥0. The aperture adds its interval on the last sum, separately at each setting. At a positive distance from every prefix boundary, the corresponding expected number of prefixes is exponentially large in sqrt(N). Independence of branches bounds relative variances by the reciprocal of those counts, so the weighted mass self-averages on fixed compact bins. For negative prefix sums the expected number of prefixes instead vanishes. The expected Gaussian weighted mass in boundary strips and tails tends to zero as the strip shrinks and the cutoff grows. This gives the two ordinary restricted normalizers B_i.

For a root with t=X_0+Ng sqrt(2 ratio)v_0 in a fixed finite interval, its scaled intensity is Q exp(c t) dt with Q=1/(g sqrt(2 pi v_0)). Its descendants have their own critical cone x_1≥0, x_1+x_2≥0. Their contribution to setting i's aperture-restricted normalizer is exp(−c t) S_i. The marked contribution is exp(−c t) K_i, adding that detector's site bands. The root's factor is common within one setting, and the two settings can have different S_i/B_i. Contributions from individual second edges vanish inside the typical cone, so the product of the marked masses has an asymptotically negligible same-second-edge term. The probability of two target roots is order N^−1. The independent remaining roots converge to the ordinary aperture-restricted masses, so the target root has to be included in each denominator.

Substitution u=exp(−ct) cancels the two numerator factors against the rare-root intensity Jacobian. The oracle evaluates the ordinary Gaussian wedge by conditioning on the total and intermediate sums. The reference instead integrates the original three edge coordinates in order, and performs the final parent integration in t rather than u. The calibrated rectangular shortcut assumes each independent increment must be nonnegative, even when a positive earlier energy fluctuation makes a negative later increment physically feasible.
