# Switched axis heat tails

Princeton's graduate problems on rotational dynamics and work motivate this original non-equilibrium calorimetry experiment. Its two baths measure heat along fixed laboratory directions while each trap can have different principal axes. [Princeton exam archive](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams) [Boundary contributions to heat fluctuations](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.91.110601)

The weighted calorimeter vector field H*X uses H=diag(weights)*A. Its symmetric part gives an endpoint quadratic form; its antisymmetric part F contributes the bulk area. Under a counting bias h the area-only tilted generator has drift A+2*h*D*F and potential quadratic form h*sym(F^T*A)+h²*F^T*D*F. The stabilizing Gaussian right eigenfunction gives a Riccati equation and a driven OU process. With its stationary covariance, the right and left Gaussian factors govern the weighting of a large physical position at each stage boundary. Add the symmetric heat contribution and apply integrability of products at the actual shared endpoints. Then take the closed-domain convex transform of the fraction-weighted eigenvalues.

The completed approximation uses the exact bulk tilt but the original stationary covariances for all junction heat, factorized from that tilt. The oracle uses the stable invariant plane of a Hamiltonian matrix. The independent reference measures the tilted midpoint heat over small steps of the exact OU transition kernel, composes Gaussian kernels by marginalization and repeated doubling, and refines the step size. It independently recovers both the large-time tilt rate and the joint endpoint integrability interval. The validator checks convergence, single-stage steady means and the identity upon subdividing one unchanged stage.
