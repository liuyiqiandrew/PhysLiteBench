# Coexisting frozen landscapes

This original problem uses competing equilibrium phases and logarithmic finite-size terms in the style of [Princeton's graduate physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams). The two phases have different depth-resolved fluctuations. The circuit cancels their extensive energies and the leading logarithmic shifts of their extreme states, leaving a controllable phase competition.

At coexisting logarithmic biases the two sets of unnormalized Gibbs jumps enter one partition function. A level with entropy s supplies the absolute Poisson tail constant (1/\sqrt{4\pi s}) after the prescribed energy centering. The outer stable phase mass also contains the fractional moment of its thermal leaf mass. Both constants affect the phase competition. The completed approximation treats each depth tail constant as one and misses relative masses. Distinct logarithmic biases lead to only one surviving phase.

The oracle integrates the joint marked Poisson cascade. The independent reference formulates the mixture as two competing stable root processes with their tilted conditional family partitions. Simulation generates the two top-weight Poisson processes independently and verifies phase proportions and the same-family/same-leaf probabilities.
