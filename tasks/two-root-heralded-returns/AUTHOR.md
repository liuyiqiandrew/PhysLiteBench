# Author analysis

This original gated thermometry experiment draws on Gaussian probability and canonical ensembles in the [Princeton graduate physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

In each finite root window the count has expectation of order N^(−1/2) with density phi_F(0) exp(ct) dt on the rescaled count. The root draws are independent. Conditioning on one in each window therefore yields two independent conditional displacements with density proportional to exp(ct) on their respective intervals. The remaining roots, conditioned to be outside those finite windows, have the same ordinary Gaussian cone mass in the limit. The ordinary support is x≥0 and x+y≥0. Interior boxes concentrate by the shared-label count bounds, negative margins are absent by first moments, and Gaussian first moments control the boundary regions and tails.

At fixed selected displacements t_0,t_1, the two tagged families each have an ordinary half-line cloud in y≥0. At setting j its Gibbs mass is exp(−ct_k) L_jk; L_jk uses the site tilt from that tag and that setting. The total diffuse mass is B_j+exp(−ct_0)L_j0+exp(−ct_1)L_j1. At a selected shared site of tag k, its finite unscaled leaf displacement d has scaled density phi_L(0) exp(cd) dd and the forced site contributes W=exp(−c(t_k+d)) in both numerators and denominators. The remaining cloud in its tag has the unaffected half-line law. An extra draw of the opposite tag contributes the appropriate restricted mass in its setting, normalized by that same full mass. Bounded probabilities and compact displacement intervals justify passage to the conditional limit.

The oracle changes the two heralded variables to uniform exp(ct_k) and the forced leaf's integration variable to exp(−cd). The reference integrates the original displacements with their probability and count densities, using independently ordered Gaussian integrals. The completed shortcut treats each heralded displacement as uniform in energy before evaluating both Gibbs laws.
