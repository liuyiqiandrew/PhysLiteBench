## Pair annihilation

Exactly one trigger photon at leading order selects C from the weak interaction. Two annihilation operators act on a normalized three-photon state with one occupied input rail per label. Every ordered assignment of the incoming photons to the two converter rails and the survivor contributes. The two conversion ports feed one monitored field. Sum amplitudes over all input-frequency partitions at the same sum frequency, all assignments and all converter ports. An independently resolved source marker weights cross terms by its overlap. The parity gate keeps or rejects the full three-rail configuration immediately before the nonlinear interaction.

With trigger frequency z and surviving fundamental frequency v, let the converted offsets be u and z−u. For each assignment permute (u,z−u,v) into the input order and evaluate its Gaussian spectral amplitude. Multiply by the three spatial matrix elements up to the converter, the phases of fields traversing the plate, and h(u,z−u). Gaussian integration over u produces a two-variable complex amplitude in (z,v). A bucket trigger traces over z, a spectral band restricts its integration range, and a coherent filter projects the amplitude onto a normalized spectral mode. In a gated arrival-time measurement Fourier transform the amplitude in z, then integrate intensity over the arrival-time interval. The remaining v is always traced out. The tap keeps the trigger line separate from the surviving fundamental detector. The ratio of the exit-bank fringe to accepted intensity implements the conditional observable.

The oracle uses Gaussian Schur complements and the normal cumulative distribution for the remaining finite window. The independent reference instead forms the symmetrized wavefunction on a grid of u−z/2, z and v. It explicitly propagates spatial amplitudes through the plate and applies the parity projector, pair annihilation, final mixer and receiver. A direct time Fourier sum handles the arrival-time receiver. This calculation uses no Gaussian elimination formula.
