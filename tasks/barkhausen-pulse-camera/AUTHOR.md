# Reciprocal domain-wall scans

The device revisits the same quenched pinning field after a reset. Conditional on the two first stops, the overlap segment runs between two moving linear boundaries. Its left endpoint touches the upper boundary at the reverse stop and its right endpoint touches the lower boundary at the forward stop. Outside pieces cancel from the conditional optical ratio by the spatial Markov property. The endpoint law is the entrance and exit flux of the killed bridge, obtained by approaching both endpoints from the interior. Giving specified velocities zero merely at those two positions would admit trajectories which stopped earlier.

For a linear strip of width W(x), the unrestricted bridge divided by W after subtracting its lower edge is a unit-interval Brownian bridge at time ∫dx/W². Its Gaussian covariance and endpoint interpolation establish the change of variables. The clock increment from a to x is (x−a)/(W(a)W(x)). Killing carries over under this monotone coordinate change. The distinction between the two endpoint fluxes gives alternating and positive derivative coefficients for the sine modes. Inserting the speed windows in the actual spatial order yields both the joint and single-gate masses.

An independent finite-difference reference propagates the killed pinning-force density in laboratory distance, including the drift and compression caused by the moving boundaries. It injects beside the upper edge and reads flux beside the lower edge; it never uses the time-transformed bridge or its eigenfunctions. On a 160-cell spatial mesh the worst error across 21 measurements is 0.000123. The 240-cell worked check gives 0.54129501 against 0.54129732. Reflecting the strip and exchanging both scanners preserves the readings. The completed shortcut has the exact marginal law for the second gate under both first-stop conditions, but samples that gate on a separate strip. It misses the three groups by 0.0821, 0.0904 and 0.0639 against tolerance 0.004.
