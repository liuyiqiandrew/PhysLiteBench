# Reciprocal domain-wall scans

A strip of length L has a frozen random pinning force F(x), anchored to zero at both ends. Its ensemble is a zero-mean Gaussian Brownian bridge with covariance 2D[min(x,y)−xy/L]. A fresh strip is drawn for each two-scan measurement. Resetting the magnetic wall preserves the strip's entire force profile. D is the common unknown spatial diffusivity in [0.38,0.70].

In the forward scan, the wall starts at x=0 and advances right with speed u₊−k₊x+F(x), until that speed first reaches zero. The reverse scan starts at x=L and advances left with positive speed u₋−k₋(L−x)−F(x), again stopping at its first zero. The same signed force acts in the laboratory frame in both runs. A wall that reaches the end exits the strip.

The spectrometer selects materials whose two stop distances are s₊ measured from the left and s₋ measured from the right. Both are positive and smaller than L. Resolve the regular joint density by shrinking distance bins at fixed values of the other settings. The two visited regions overlap. Define a=L−s₋ and b=s₊; thus 0<a<b<L. Every optical measurement is strictly inside (a,b). A force profile that would have stopped a wall earlier does not pass the size selection, even if its velocity formula later becomes positive.

An optical gate [x, direction, lo, hi] passes when the speed for that scan at laboratory coordinate x lies between lo and hi. Endpoints have zero probability. Each optical measurement is passive. The two gates are listed in acquisition order; their coordinates can occur in either spatial order. Within the sample already selected by the two stop distances, return the fraction passing gate two **conditional on passing gate one**.

The input fields are `length`, `walls=[[u₊,k₊],[u₋,k₋]]`, `sizes=[s₊,s₋]` and `gates` in acquisition order. Direction is `forward` or `reverse`. The common speed range at x in the overlap is [0,W(x)], where W(x)=u₊+u₋−k₊x−k₋(L−x). Distances, forces and speeds use reduced laboratory units. Operating records have L in [2.5,3.8], u in [1,1.52], k in [0.18,0.53], a/L in [0.18,0.34], b/L in [0.66,0.82] and W(x) in [0.45,2.65] throughout the overlap. Gate coordinates are between 0.25 and 0.75 of the way from a to b, separated by at least 0.15(b−a). Both speed edges lie between 0.08 W(x) and 0.92 W(x); each window spans at least 0.18 W(x).

For D=0.55, L=3, walls [[1.25,0.25],[1.18,0.48]], sizes [2.24,2.20], and gates [[1.35,"forward",0.25,0.65],[1.74,"reverse",0.30,0.78]], the conditional fraction is 0.54129732. Interchanging the two gates gives 0.44885312.

Calibration records have an `input` with `length` and a coordinate `x` strictly inside the strip. The `value` is the unconditional force variance there, rounded to nine decimals. Implement Model.fit(records), store D in self.diffusivity and return self. Model.predict(inputs) returns a finite real NumPy vector in input order, including an empty vector for an empty list. The calibration maximum absolute error must be below 0.000002. The two-forward, two-reverse and mixed-direction gate sets each require maximum absolute probability error below 0.004. The starter uses an unrestricted bridge between the observed stopping forces.
