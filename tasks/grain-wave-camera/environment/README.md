# Long-track toppling camera

A finite connected undirected track graph has junctions 0 through sites−1 and one drain labelled −1. A track [i,j,l] is a chain of Nl identical nearest-neighbor bonds from i to j, with Nl−1 ordinary internal sites. Different tracks meet only at their ends. A bond carries one grain. The drain absorbs each grain it receives. Take N to infinity through multiples of four. Track lengths l are integers in [1,4]. Graphs have three to seven nondrain junctions, up to eleven tracks, distinct ends and no repeated track. Each junction is linked to the drain through the network.

A microscopic site with degree d topples when its height is at least d. A toppling removes d grains and adds one grain to each neighbor. Obtain the stationary recurrent ensemble by adding single grains at random microscopic sites, stabilizing after each addition. Equivalently each stable recurrent configuration has equal prior probability. Each measurement starts with such a configuration and adds one grain at the chosen nondrain drive junction. Discard an addition that causes no wave.

Start a wave by toppling the drive once at round zero. Hold the drive fixed while all other unstable sites topple in parallel, one round per step, until none remains. If the drive is unstable again, start a new wave with its clock reset to zero. Otherwise that wave ends the avalanche. Each microscopic site topples at most once within a wave. Record every emitted wave from the long sequence of independent preparations. For class `all` retain every wave. For `last` retain only the final wave of each avalanche. For `port` retain a final wave when the immediately adjacent microscopic site on at least one of the listed drive tracks failed to topple in that wave. The list `ports` gives track indices, from zero, in that case. The return sensors are mounted one **microscopic bond** away from the drive for every N.

The two `pickup` entries [e,r] observe the site a fraction r of the distance along track e starting at its first listed end. Fractions are 1/4, 1/2 or 3/4. A toppling of a pickup site emits a flash at its parallel round divided by N, in macroscopic time units. The camera has one initially open shutter per wave, independent of the pile. Its closure time is exponential with unknown rate γ in [0.09,0.24]. Keep a wave only if the first pickup flashes through the open shutter at a time in the inclusive interval `rounds`. Report the conditional probability that the second pickup also flashed through that very same shutter and that its flash time minus the first flash time lies in the inclusive interval `delay`. That difference can be negative. The two pickups can lie on the same track. The selections and conditional probability are defined at finite N **before** taking the limit. Operating settings have a nonempty conditional ensemble in the limit after accounting for the rarity of final waves.

At γ=0.15, use five junctions, drive 0, and tracks [[0,1,1],[0,2,3],[0,3,2],[1,2,1],[2,3,2],[1,4,2],[3,4,1],[4,−1,2],[2,−1,1]]. For pickup [[0,0.5],[1,0.5]], rounds [0,8] and delay [−1.125,2.125], class all gives 0.47750136, last gives 0.24001667, and port with ports [0,2] gives 0.45511198.

Calibration sends a light pulse at a known nonnegative macroscopic `test_round` and measures the open fraction. In data/calibration.json each record has `input` and dimensionless `value`, rounded to nine decimals. Model.fit(records) stores the rate in self.rate and returns self. Model.predict(inputs) returns a finite numeric NumPy vector in input order, including an empty vector for an empty list. Maximum absolute errors must be below 0.000002 for calibration and below 0.004 in each held-out wave class.
