# Tethered polymer loop at fixed radius

The closed loop is the positive-reach limit of a short boundary-to-boundary chord. [Beliaev and Johansson Viklund](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.5206) give the enclosed-point measures and the radial tail for this model. The single-dye shell formula follows directly by mapping the half disk to the upper half-plane using z/(1+z²/R²), differentiating the enclosed mass at fixed dye position, and dividing by the radial density. All gates and wall contacts apply to the same loop before measuring its radius.

The oracle removes infinitesimal wall slits by their conformal displacement and derivative weights. The pinned bubble has weight two at the origin. Each probe transforms as a capacity with weight two at its wall site. The oracle differentiates the two resulting confined masses with respect to the corral radius holding laboratory positions and probe heights fixed. This yields the conditional enclosure probability at the measured maximum.

The private reference instead starts from an ordinary chord with two distinct endpoints, first at gap e and then at −e by reflection. Its avoidance weight uses both endpoint derivatives and their transformed separation, all with exponent 5/8. The two-dye angular function is obtained by solving its transport equation. Finite positive slits use the full square-root mapping in reverse probe order, and independent positive-capacity, gap and shell limits recover the bubble response. Every reference reading agrees with the oracle to 4e−7 or better. The 24 gated settings also pass reflection, physical scaling and wall exchange. The completed shortcut has the correct exact joint contact and enclosure laws under a cap on loop size. Conditioning instead on the measured radius band changes the largest readings by 0.248, 0.392 and 0.213 across the three groups. Both implementations recover the calibration. Solver and verifier time limits match the other tasks in this expansion.
