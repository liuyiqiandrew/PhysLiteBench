# Tethered polymer loop

A simple polymer chord runs through the upper half-plane from the wall coordinate 0 to a nearby wall coordinate e>0. The chord and the short wall segment bound a pocket. Its continuum law is chordal SLE(8/3). For events requiring a nonzero excursion, the pinned bubble measure μ is the limit of the chord probabilities divided by e² as e decreases to zero. The detector forms ratios of these measures. This defines the equilibrium loop used here.

The radius of a loop is its farthest Euclidean distance from its tether at the origin. The camera records loops whose maximum is in [R,R+dR], taking dR down to zero. Every wall site x in the input must also fire. It fires when that same loop intersects the vertical segment from x to x+i√(2a). Use the leading coefficient in the product of the specified slit capacities a as each tends to zero. The gate, radius and wall observations are passive. The `inside` gate requires the first dye to be inside the pocket. Return the conditional probability that the second dye is inside. The `off` gate has no first-dye condition and occurs only in calibration with no wall probes.

The following laws specify the continuum model without outside data. In the unbounded upper half-plane let K(z)=−Im(1/z) and s=|z−w|²/|z−conj(w)|². The measure of enclosing z is K(z)²/4. The measure of enclosing both z and w is K(z)K(w)G(s)/4. Here 3s(1−s)G′=(s−1)+(s+1)G, G(0)=1, G(1)=0. The measure of reaching beyond R is 5/(8R²). A conformal map f from a subdomain onto the upper half-plane with f(0)=0 and positive f′(0) carries the measure of loops staying in the subdomain and enclosing specified dyes to f′(0)² times the unbounded measure of the transformed enclosure event. This rule applies to a half disk and to domains with boundary-attached slits. All dye positions and probes describe the original laboratory frame.

Each input provides `radius` R in [2,3.2], the two `dyes` as pixel pairs [X,Y], a `walls` list, and the `gate`. A dye's physical position is X+iλY. Fit the common λ in [0.78,1.22]. In the operating window |X|≤0.60R, 0.18R≤Y≤0.58R, the dye centers are at least 0.22R apart in pixels and both stay below 0.91R in physical radius. There are zero, one or two wall probes of absolute coordinate 0.58 to 1.18. A two-probe record uses opposite sides of the tether. All lengths use the horizontal pixel as unit. For an open camera the single dye probability on a radius-R shell is (4/5) Im(w)² (1/|w|²−1/R²).

For λ=1, dyes (−0.42,0.84) and (0.94,0.66), and radius 2.25, the first-dye gate gives second-dye probability 0.117654 with no probes, 0.088588 with a probe at −0.74, and 0.739682 with probes at −0.74 and 1.04. Selecting all smaller radii in the last example instead gives 0.610120.

Implement `Model.fit(rows)` to set `self.aspect` and return self. Entries in data/calibration.json contain an `input` and a probability `value`, rounded to eight decimals. `Model.predict(inputs)` returns a finite numeric NumPy vector in input order, including length zero. The supplied starter fits the single-dye response. The maximum absolute error must be below 0.00002 on calibration, and below 0.004 on each of the zero-, one- and two-probe gated sets.
