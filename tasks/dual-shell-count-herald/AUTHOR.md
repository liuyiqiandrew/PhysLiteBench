# Author analysis

This original count-heralded spectrometer is inspired by microcanonical sampling and joint fluctuation measurements in the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

At the centered energy U the ordinary marked sites converge to a Poisson process with intensity exp(cU) phi_total(0) phi_bridge(R) dU dR on R≥0. For R on a compact positive margin each family contributes a very small expected count, while the independent family population concentrates. The probability of an unmodified family energy below the negative margin vanishes. Shrinking bridge strips and Gaussian tails have vanishing expected counts. In the limiting ordinary process, two distinct sites have distinct families. A site in the intersection contributes to both counts, so the herald uses the joint three-class Poisson law for only setting 0, only setting 1, and both.

A family with unmodified displacement t in the supplied finite window has count intensity phi_family(0)*exp(ct) dt/sqrt(N). Force a single such family when computing the numerator by summing over its actual label. Its independent sites contribute a second Poisson process in the union of the spectral windows, with R tending to zero and density phi_site(0)*exp(c(U−t))dU. Its descendant count joins the same herald, including possible visible sites outside the requested marks. The remainder of the sample retains its ordinary limit.

For a given ordered event, insert one atom per distinct requested label. For each marked atom its visibility vector removes its count contribution from the target herald before asking the uninserted processes to satisfy those remaining counts. A repeated site contributes only one atom, even though it supplies both draws. An outside atom uses the ordinary pool and is necessarily outside the forced family. For observed counts (n0,n1) the uniform draw denominator is n0*n1 with one further count factor if there is an outside draw. The conditioning probability is the ordinary joint herald probability, which is positive since all three visibility regions exist at small positive R. Bounded conditional probabilities and compact t give the rare-event limit.

The oracle splits the envelope of spectral boundaries at line crossings and integrates the tilted Gaussian in each piece. The reference quadratures the lines and obtains the count probabilities by explicit convolution. The completed shortcut separates the two observed shell counts when computing the herald, while keeping the joint rare-event counting.
