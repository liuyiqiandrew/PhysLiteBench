# Critical glass bridge marks

This original experiment combines Gaussian fluctuations, quenched equilibrium, and finite-size scaling from [Princeton's graduate preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams). [Bovier and Kurkova's GREM paper](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIHPB_2004__40_4_439_0.pdf) supplies the Gaussian bridge constraint for critical same-slope levels. The problem varies each entropy at order N to the power −1/2 and applies a field to the resulting bridge variables.

At a frozen root, the three inner levels have a common limiting slope. At fixed total energy, the two residuals are a Gaussian bridge. To produce a near-extreme leaf, each ancestor must actually exist. Counting ancestors with that prefix energy gives the lower support bounds −prefix(detuning)/critical_inverse_temperature on the two coordinates. The asymptotic inner extremes have distinct first inner branches. A fixed field exponentially tilts this truncated Gaussian by the critical inverse temperature. Independent leaf marks survive the child weight bias given the selected outer family. The child partition is sampled with the negative outer index in the Poisson–Dirichlet law.

The oracle uses conditional Gaussian rectangle quadrature and the partition formula. The reference independently uses insertion probabilities and weighted Sobol integration plus direct finite-size Gaussian tail bounds on the expected number of additional extreme siblings. The completed approximation handles the partition statistics and field tilt but lets the Gaussian range extend through the forbidden region.
