# Author analysis

This original thin-gate experiment is inspired by critical fluctuations and constrained phase-space integrals in the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

The ordinary critical cloud has cumulative support x≥0 and S=x+y≥0. At center b>0 the limiting gate line x+m*y=(1−m)*b cuts a segment of this cone parameterized by 0≤x≤b. The mass divided by delta is its tilted Gaussian integral along x, times the gate width divided by m. At b=0 use v=x/delta and w=S/delta. The ordinary scaled region is v,w≥0 and the gate clips (1−m)*v+m*w between its positive endpoints. Its area is the difference of their squares divided by 2*m*(1−m). The mass divided by delta² is this area times both Gaussian densities at zero. These gate masses concentrate by strict sampled margins in the appropriate coordinates. Even margins of fixed positive width in the rescaled tip variables have exponential count factors exp(constant*N^(1/4)). Boundary strips of shrinking relative width have vanishing normalized Gaussian mass, and negative prefix margins have vanishing counts.

At the prescribed rare-site line let x=b+delta*u. The line has S=(exponent*log(N)/c+t)/sqrt(N), so each observed gate tends to (1−m)*u. Both gates admit only their common address interval up to a null boundary. Its scaled count density is phi_F(b)*phi_L(−b)*exp(ct) du dt. The root has strict positive x on surviving windows, including x=delta*u at b=0 since gate endpoints are positive. Its other sites add vanishing mass relative to the gate normalization. The forced label contributes exp(−ct+(a_j0−a_j1)*b) to that normalization after division by N^(−exponent). Both Gibbs ratios retain it.

The oracle integrates the regular segment by x and measures the gate tip using its triangular area. The reference integrates the segment in S and the tip by slicing the rescaled cone. The completed shortcut uses independent x,y≥0 at the tip, producing a different gate area.
