# Author analysis

This original statistical spectroscopy task draws on thermal selection and counting in the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

The expected scaled count of roots at finite displacement (z=X+Na v_F+\mathrm{depth}\sqrt N) approaches (G_F(-\mathrm{depth})e^{cz}dz) by exact Gaussian tilting. The probability of two such roots vanishes at the requested scaling. Remove those roots from the ordinary background. Their window lies strictly below the root feasibility boundary. The other families give the same ordinary Gibbs masses (B_j) on the tilted Gaussian cone (x\geq0, x+y\geq0).

Condition on the chosen root at (z). The raw expected child count in finite (dt) is asymptotic to (e^{c\,\mathrm{depth}\sqrt N}G_L(\mathrm{depth})e^{c(t-z)}dt/\sqrt N). The quenched retention probability cancels both large factors and produces a Poisson process with intensity \(K(z)e^{ct}dt\), where (K(z)=\mathrm{retention}\,G_L(\mathrm{depth})e^{-cz}\). Disjoint retained energy intervals are asymptotically independent. This convergence and the conditional Gibbs readout hold uniformly on compact intervals, with bounded conditional probabilities.

For one point at t in gate 0 and no others there, its density is (e^{-\Lambda_0(z)}K(z)e^{ct}dt\), with (\Lambda_0(z)=K(z)\int_{\mathrm{gate}_0}e^{cv}dv\). Require t in both gates and the line window. At this site the scalar Gibbs weight (w_j(t)=\exp[-ct+\mathrm{depth}(h_{j1}-h_{j0})]\) uses (h_{ji}=c\,\mathrm{field}_{ji}-\mathrm{window}_j\). The first denominator is (B_0+w_0(t)). The second is (B_1+w_1(t)+S\), where S is the sum of weights of all independent Poisson points in gate 1 outside gate 0. The event uses both selected weights in the numerator. Requiring the outside draw changes the second denominator's power from one to two and multiplies by (B_1).

The compound Poisson transform is (\mathbb E[e^{-s S}]=\exp[-K(z)\int (1-e^{-s w_1(v)})e^{cv}dv]\) over the gate difference. The reciprocal moment is its Laplace integral against (s^{r-1}e^{-s(B_1+w_1(t))}\). The oracle uses a generalized Gauss–Laguerre rule. The reference uses adaptive integration of survival in the Laplace variable and integrates the bulk conditional on the family coordinate. The completed shortcut drops the gate 1-only population from the second normalizer after observing one point in gate 0.
