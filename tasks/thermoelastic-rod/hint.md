The clamps constrain the spatial mean strain, while axial force balance
requires a spatially uniform total stress. When the viscous coefficient varies,
the reaction stress must be determined from the local Kelvin–Voigt law and
the rate of that global constraint. Projecting an unconstrained local strain
rate to zero ordinary mean can preserve the clamp without enforcing uniform
stress. Couple the resulting strain rate to the reversible heat balance.
