# Spectrometer with a shared differential reference

The strong-disorder singlet spectrum is a marked renewal process. A common pair can give two clicks on opposite spins at exactly one true formation coordinate, while clicks on different pairs give genuinely distinct lines. An oppositely driven spectrograph and Ramsey electronics share a pinned or continuously varying detuning. The finite-aperture conditioning is part of the observable. For the underlying random-singlet waiting law, see [Fagotti, Calabrese and Moore, Phys. Rev. B 83, 045110 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.83.045110).

## Detector measures

Let A(t,u) be the raw source density per ordinary left/right pair and B(t) the common-line density per single true formation coordinate. Each is computed both with and without the Ramsey arm weight. Changing from the original two coordinates to physical center c and half-splitting d gives ordinary density 2A(c+d,c−d) dc dd. The common component is B(c) dc at d=0. An accepted detuning adds to d and applies the phase exp(−iτξ) to the complex arm trace on that shot. For the beat channel, use the preimage of the slit on both signs. The negative and positive intervals join at zero when appropriate.

For a continuous measured splitting the ordinary component includes the integral over all compatible true splittings, plus the locked reference's evaluation of the ordinary density. A genuinely common line supplies an additional term through the density of the unlocked detuning. A nonzero locked mass can leave an atom at its displayed splitting. Resolving exactly that value selects its leading singular weight when the underlying common-line intensity is positive. For a finite slit the locked atom is included whenever the slit covers it. Normalize by the independently transformed acceptance measure.

The oracle integrates in true half-splitting and inserts the exact characteristic of the admitted uniform subinterval for the Ramsey phase. It splits quadrature at band boundaries, true coincidence, slit edges and detuning breakpoints. The private reference instead integrates finite measured center and slit apertures in the original true left and right times. The common contribution is a separate integral over one true time. It evolves the underlying source with the direct marked-bond density coefficients (a+b x)exp(−x) and builds signal rotations in a two-spin wavefunction. It extrapolates finite-aperture probabilities before forming the reported ratio. It imports neither the sharp source helper nor the oracle.

For the uniform band in the worked example, the boundary flux F_z(t) after a fresh crossing is exp(−3t/2) 2 sinh(t√(5+4z)/2)/√(5+4z) when earlier crossings have weight z. The ordinary counting function is G_z(t)=exp(−3t/2)[cosh(t√(5+4z)/2)+3 sinh(t√(5+4z)/2)/√(5+4z)]. At a fixed arm weight m, the common-line branch is proportional to (m/4) F_(m/4)(1) G_m(1). For a physical half-splitting of magnitude d the distinct-line branch is (m²/8)F_(m/4)(1−d)F_(m/2)(2d)G_m(1−d). Integrating these expressions against the stated uniform dither gives −0.436157362603 at m=−1 after normalization by m=+1. Discarding the common-line source gives +0.652596353242.
