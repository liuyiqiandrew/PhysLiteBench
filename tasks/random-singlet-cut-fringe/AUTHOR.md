# Ballistic carrier across a random-singlet cut

One spin crosses a disordered singlet chain from left to right. Its encounter order depends on the spatial geometry of the original sites. The magnetic field applied after each exchange ties the carrier to a lab-frame axis, so the carrier maps of separate singlets need not commute. The two-cut spectrum comes from the strong-disorder model in the public instrument. For the single-cut fixed-point background, see [Fagotti, Calabrese and Moore, Phys. Rev. B 83, 045110 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.83.045110).

## Spatial circuits

A nearest-active-neighbor singlet cannot cross an existing singlet arc in the original spatial embedding. Across one cut, successive singlets instead enclose all previously formed arcs crossing that cut. Before the two cuts merge, there are at most two one-sided singlets because each removes an original central spin. If two occur at the same cut, the second wraps the earlier carrier circuit. If they occur on opposite sides, the carrier completes both couplings on the left pair before entering the right pair, irrespective of which formed first. Every later spanning singlet encloses both earlier one-sided circuits.

For each pair, the supplied linear transform M wraps an arbitrary carrier map in its exchange and precession gates and traces that singlet. Write C=reshape(M vec(I)) for the isolated singlet's carrier map. If E has already been generated by an earlier, opposite-side arc, a later right arc instead yields CE. A later left arc yields EC. An isolated or same-side arc produces reshape(M vec(E)). Each spanning arc also wraps E this way. The oracle keeps a linear record with three possible prior sides: none, left and right. The same disorder realization supplies the geometry and the three optical herald streams. Contraction with the prepared carrier, launch fields and analyzer gives each unnormalized Ramsey-arm trace; the measured coherence is normalized by the mean of the two retained diagonal traces.

The two-cut disorder generator starts with either one or two central active spins. The untouched inner exchange has activation hazard exp(μ). Each unmodified outer boundary crosses at rate one. A pending common bond remembers that inner exchange if the two boundaries disappear before its activation. An ordinary regenerated common cut has the two-stage waiting density with rates (3±√5)/2. The public helper implements this continuum flow and separate click counts for the three geometries.

## Validation

The independent reference computes local transforms by inserting carrier matrix units in a literal three-spin Hilbert space. It remembers the prior one-sided arc in its canonical bond populations, applies disjoint carrier channels by explicit left or right matrix multiplication, and uses the continuum coefficients of (a+b x)exp(−x) to transport common crossings. The oracle uses the public two-stage generator and tensor multiplication. The author validator compares both implementations at fixed parameters on all 18 hidden scans. A separate five-spin contraction establishes the worked spatial witness: the accepted disjoint arcs give 0.5 V, while one alternative nested ordering would give −0.25 V. The model that nests every pair mixes the two allowed formation orders and gives 0.125 V.

There are 12 calibration scans. The single-center group has no disjoint arcs. The mixed and opposite-arc groups test their geometry and the order of noncommuting fields. The starter nests every pair. A second control correctly identifies disjoint arcs but sends the carrier through the right one first. Both reproduce the single-center group and fail the multiarc groups. The validator is `scripts/validate_random_singlet_cut_fringe.py`; add `--generate` to write the fixtures. The native solve and verifier allowances are 1,800 and 180 seconds.
