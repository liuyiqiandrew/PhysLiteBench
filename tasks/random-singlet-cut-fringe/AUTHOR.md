# Two first lines in a random-singlet spectrum

Two spin reporters monitor opposite sides of a disordered XX chain. A crossing singlet has one formation energy even if the two spatially separated reporters both detect it. The energy record therefore mixes ordinary no-click probabilities, continuous single and two-line densities, and a singular coincident contribution on the diagonal. The signal is measured on a matched realization carrying independent spin states. The strong-disorder fixed-point rules define the quantitative spectrum; the energy-selective optical apparatus is idealized. For the marked-bond renewal background, see [Fagotti, Calabrese and Moore, Phys. Rev. B 83, 045110 (2011)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevB.83.045110).

For a spanning pair, write n_L,n_R for the effective spin analyzer directions. Singlet marginals are unpolarized, and the probability of two registered clicks is η_Lη_R(1−n_L·n_R)/4. Together with marginal click rates η_i/2 this determines four joint marks. A one-sided pair has only its own channel. The signal pair contributes an independent coherent factor ⟨ψ₀|ψ₁⟩ conditional on the same energy, geometry and sublattice.

## Sharp-line propagation

The two-cut dynamical states keep the number and arrangement of central spins and the common-cut renewal stage. The untouched interior exchange activates at rate exp(μ); each fresh boundary contributes rate one. After an initial one-sided decimation the adjacent boundary density is E₁∗Eₖ with Eₖ(x)=k exp(−kx), k=exp(μ). If the other boundary disappears before that inner exchange activates the resulting common bond has density E₁∗E₁∗Eₖ. Upon activation it enters the ordinary common renewal. The latter has interarrival distribution equal to a sum of two exponentials with rates (3±√5)/2. Left-only and common crossings flip the exterior left sign.

Between required line coordinates, keep only transitions whose marks are silent on every detector with a future first line or no line at all. A detector already observed is unconstrained. At each distinct required coordinate insert the rate kernel for exactly the necessary new clicks while respecting the other still-silent channels. If the coordinates coincide, insert a single transition registering both. Distinct events falling in the same shrinking window have smaller order in width. Divide the complex signal trace by a second propagation with the optical factors set to one before taking the displayed quadrature.

The independent calculation uses actual finite-width gates, four click flags and the direct common density (a+b x)exp(−x). It integrates spin probabilities using rotated Bloch axes and signal amplitudes from a two-spin wavefunction. It never inserts a sharp spectral line. Reducing all window widths at fixed centers gives the tabulated limits. Extrapolation uses widths h, h/2 and h/4 and cancels terms linear and quadratic in h.

## Checks and controls

For the worked source, the common-cut boundary flux after a reset, weighted by a factor z at each earlier crossing, is exp(−3t/2) 2 sinh(t√(5+4z)/2)/√(5+4z). The two reporters always click together. An unregistered earlier pair thus contributes z=1/2 to normalization and z=−1/2 to the arm trace. The detected pair has signed weight −1/2. At t=1 this gives the stated line density and conditional fringe.

The validator compares the independent window limits to the sharp kernels for all 24 unseen runs and checks the different powers of resolution on equal and unequal readings. The public Poisson control keeps the two-spin correlation and loses the shared two-cut spectrum. A second exact-spectrum control substitutes independent spin outcomes at the ends of a common singlet. It fails the silent, separated and coincident readings. Twelve empty-scan calibrations identify the visibility.
