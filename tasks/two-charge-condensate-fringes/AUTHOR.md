# Two-charge condensate fringes

Ideal gas state counting and coherent internal rotations in Princeton's graduate preliminary exams motivate this original charge-constrained preparation. Two gapped modes carry thermal charges, while the low-energy modes share the residual charge and particle density. [Princeton exam archive](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams)

The extensive saturation occupations are the usual Bose integrals at zero chemical potentials for this condensed support. The residual densities fix x_plus-x_minus separately in each charged pair. Let s_1 and s_2 be the two pair sums. Their lower limits are the magnitudes of the residual charges and their upper sum is the total excess density. The neutral occupancy fills the remainder. The canonical measure on that triangle has a constant counting Jacobian and a weight from the specified finite preparation energy. In a shot with fixed macroscopic occupations, the large-volume optical amplitudes use the square roots of those occupations with one free phase per occupied mode. Averaging a nonlinear detector requires the joint thermal triangle and pulse phase distribution.

The completed approximation averages the occupation vector first and then uses coherent pulse statistics of a single number preparation with those mean occupations. It reproduces calibration and mean ports but removes the distribution of populations at fixed two-charge totals. The oracle uses scrambled low-discrepancy integration. The independent reference uses a different sample and triangle transform, and checks population averages by direct canonical counting on a finite grid and the exact unpulsed marginal.
