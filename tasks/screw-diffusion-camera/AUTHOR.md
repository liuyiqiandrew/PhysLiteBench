# Screw diffusion camera, revisions 1–3

A Brownian rigid body's mobility can couple translation to rotation. The position tracked by a camera can also differ from the material origin at which the diffusion coefficients were specified. Both effects matter when optical selection weights the orientations of the same particle at two times. Translational and rotational cross-correlations and their dependence on the chosen tracking point are derived by Cichocki, Ekiel-Jeżewska and Wajnryb. The apparatus here specifies its complete positive diffusion matrix through the six independent Brownian rigid motions. [Brownian motion of a particle with arbitrary shape](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03747)

## Backward equation at the tag

Let u=Rᵀe be the camera direction in the body frame. Its invariant initial distribution is uniform on the sphere. If the body rotates about its j-th body axis, u evolves by the vector field Jⱼ=(u×eⱼ)·∇. For the tagged coordinate the same rotation moves the observed point along the body vector cⱼ=hⱼ+eⱼ×b. This follows by differentiating x+Rb along the specified finite screw. Thus in the spatial Fourier sector exp(ikz), the joint rotational channel acts by

    Aⱼ(k) = Jⱼ + ik u·cⱼ.

The backward generator in that sector is

    L(k) = ν ∑ⱼ [rⱼ Aⱼ(k)² − dⱼ k² uⱼ²].

The square acts on the full product. Its first-order part in k includes Jⱼ(u·cⱼ) in addition to 2(u·cⱼ)Jⱼ. Dropping that derivative changes the tagged drift. The free rotational correlator along principal body axis j is exp[−ν(∑r−rⱼ)t], since the other two rotational vector fields each square to −1 on uⱼ. The calibration scale follows from a linear fit to the logarithms.

Write W₀ and W₁ for multiplication by the two gate acceptance probabilities. In the uniform initial ensemble, the complex numerator is the spherical average of

    W₀ exp[t₁ L(k₁)] W₁ exp[t₂ L(k₂)] 1.

The denominator is the spherical average of W₀ exp[t₁ L(0)] W₁. Taking the real part of their ratio times exp(−i phase) gives the reported fringe. The two intervals share a single intermediate orientation. A product of separately angle-averaged characteristic functions misses this memory.

## Independent checks

The oracle applies this backward generator in complex spherical harmonics through degree seven. Angular momentum matrices implement Jⱼ. It forms coordinate products with an extra angular degree before truncation. The private reference propagates the original material origin with full finite positive and negative screw rotations, including the exact translation integrated along each curved body-axis path. It differences the two transfers and projects the result at degree nine. All three tag offsets enter through explicit endpoint phase factors. The reference does not shift the mobility tensor to the tag or use differential angular-momentum matrices.

Seed 305791 gives four families of seven readings each. The origin family measures screw motion itself. The tagged family adds a lever arm. The quadrature family selects the imaginary part of the conditional scattering signal. The reversal family gives the successive displacements opposite gradient signs. Checks include mirror reversal, zero-gradient normalization, refinement to degree eleven and halving the finite angular step from 0.003 to 0.0015 radians. The largest difference between oracle and frozen target is 2.6 × 10⁻⁷. The calibration and fringe absolute tolerances are 0.000002 and 0.003.

The completed shortcut gives the material origin and body orientation independent noises with their correct marginal diffusion tensors. It retains the geometric motion of the off-origin tag, both orientation gates and the shared intermediate orientation. It fits the rotational calibration and errs by 0.0217 to 0.0868 across the four families. The public starter instead treats the tag as an isotropic Gaussian random walk with its exact orientation-averaged instantaneous variance. It fits the same calibration and predicts zero for every sine quadrature. The public worked example exposes this approximation.

Run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python scripts/validate_screw_diffusion_camera.py` to reproduce the stored observables and compare the models. Add `--generate` to update the data intentionally. Both the training and grading images set a single BLAS thread. The 1,800-second solve and 180-second verifier limits match recent physical-failure panels.

## Motor-current and synchronized protocols, revision 2

For the dimensionless three-state process the forward and backward rates are a=1+B and b=1−B. Exponential tilting by the net count preserves their product. The tilted generator has a constant principal eigenvector by cyclic symmetry. Its driven rates satisfy a′−b′=f, a′b′=ab. This same result follows by conditioning the two Poisson jump counts on their difference. The motor velocities have zero mean under the uniform stationary distribution for every tilt. The slow velocity noise also has zero cross covariance with the scalar jump-count fluctuation by the three-state rotation symmetry. Fixing the net count therefore leaves the same leading motion as the driven process. Only the leading fringe is read in this protocol. [Chetrite and Touchette](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.5157)

Let A and B denote the two body-screw derivative operators acting on the Fourier-transformed tag displacement. The motor acts in microscopic state j through Vⱼ=1.5√2(cA+sB). With Π the uniform stationary projection and Q the generator of the appropriate jump process, set H=(Π−Q)⁻¹−Π. Elimination of the fast motor gives the extra backward generator ν Σᵢⱼ Vᵢ Hᵢⱼ Vⱼ/3. The antisymmetric part of H multiplies the commutator of rigid motions and thus contributes an actual drift. On the sine and cosine modes let d=3(a′+b′)/2 and c=√3(a′−b′)/2. The two pieces are 2.25ν[d(A²+B²)+c(AB−BA)]/(d²+c²). Discarding the commutator preserves the symmetric translational and rotational noise. [Deuschel, Orenshtein and Perkowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.09819)

For the synchronized differential reading the jump counts are unfiltered. If u is a homogenized backward expectation, the state-dependent first correction at the starting boundary is ε Σⱼ Hᵢⱼ Vⱼu. This follows by matching the order 1/ε terms in the three-state backward equation. The initial state changes this term in both the gated numerator and the gate acceptance. Their quotient must be differentiated. The finite optical gates and later interval contribute common first-order terms that cancel in the difference of starting states.

The physical solution evaluates at the tag and uses the closed sine/cosine coefficients. The private reference advances finite rigid screws at the material origin, forms the three-state Poisson inverse and retains the tag phases at the endpoints. The validator independently evolves the original three coupled finite-ε equations at ε=0.025 and 0.0125, before taking their difference and extrapolating on two preparations. The public synchronized example gives −0.10982. The eight current and eight synchronized reference readings agree with the oracle to 0.000010 against the 0.003 threshold. The completed symmetric-diffusion approximation misses the two groups by 0.00860 and 0.15724. The four original solvent-only families and rotational calibration also run in this revision.

## Frame-return experiments, revision 3

The rigid-frame record continuously defines a point in SU(2) above the relative body orientation. A shrinking return aperture in SO(3) selects the two points above the identity. The central element −1 distinguishes a 2π rotation from a 4π rotation. The quaternion record supplies the sign directly. Its spin-interferometer interpretation uses the standard 4π periodicity observed by Rauch et al. [Verification of coherent spinor rotation of fermions](https://doi.org/10.1016/0375-9601(75)90798-7)

Let T₀(k₁,k₂) and T₁(k₁,k₂) denote the traces of W₀ exp[t₁L(k₁)] W₁ exp[t₂L(k₂)] on functions even and odd under reversal of the full orientation quaternion. These functions respectively contain all integer and all half-integer rotor representations. Integrating the orientation kernel on the diagonal gives T₀+T₁. The integral from each orientation to its negative is T₀−T₁. The common factor for a shrinking aperture cancels in the readouts. The plus and minus conditional fringes thus use the same signed combination of traces in their numerator and zero-wave denominator. The contrast includes all returns in its denominator and sign-weights the numerator, hence it uses T₁(k₁,k₂)/T₀(0,0). Apply the same detector phase as in the original fringe.

The oracle works at the tag using Wigner rotation functions for the full frame. The camera's common lab axis leaves the lab magnetic index fixed, and rank-one angular momentum coupling multiplies by the three Cartesian components of Rᵀe. In each block it diagonalizes the generator and takes the trace over all initial orientations. Two successive coordinate multipliers have one extra angular degree of padding. The reference represents rotations as symmetric tensor powers of a unit quaternion, constructs its Euler-angle quadrature independently and advances the finite positive and negative screw motions at the material origin. The tag phases on the intermediate and closed frames carry opposite coefficients k₁−k₂. The oracle uses angular cutoff nine (nine and one half for half-integer spin). The reference uses angular step 0.002 and cutoff eight (eight and one half for half-integer spin). The validator halves the angular step and raises both cutoffs.

The three additional groups share six distinct bodies and pulse settings. This allows direct comparison between positive and negative returns with every other parameter held fixed. Their worst oracle discrepancies are below 0.0000005. The control propagates the correct full-frame return distribution and uses the correct gate-weighted frequency of each sign, but treats the sign as statistically independent of the scattering phase. It passes all six earlier groups. Its maximum errors on positive returns, negative returns and contrast are 0.01247, 0.13841 and 0.02275. The public starter remains the ordinary gated solvent model.

The ungated, zero-wave sign contrast has an independent rigid-top check. For spin j take the trace of exp[−ν(t₁+t₂) Σ rᵢJᵢ²] on a single spin-j multiplet and multiply by its degeneracy 2j+1. The sum on half-integer spins divided by the sum on integer spins gives 0.85249295 for the public example. With equal wave numbers, the initial and final tag orientations coincide at closure, so the result is independent of the body coordinate of the tag.
