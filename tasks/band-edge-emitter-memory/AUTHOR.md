# Band-edge emitter memory

This original experiment uses quantum oscillations and interference ideas from Princeton's graduate preliminary exams. The emitters are placed at separations comparable to the band-edge wavelength in a weak-coupling limit. [Princeton exam archive](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams) [Bound states in waveguide quantum electrodynamics](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01300)

The scaled guide Green function for separation d is exp(-d*sqrt(z))/(2*sqrt(z)), with the upper-half-plane resolvent convention. The two emitter symmetry channels have self-energies gain²*(1±exp(-separation*sqrt(z)))/(2*sqrt(z)). The return amplitudes include both their continuum spectral densities and the residues of real poles. A locked internal pole appears where the radiative part and the corresponding frequency shift both vanish. Its atomic residue can be nontrivial even though there is no decay at that frequency. The emitter projection combines the two complex amplitudes coherently.

The completed approximation evaluates each self-energy at the bare emitter frequency and evolves with its resulting single complex frequency. It is exact for the separate isolated calibration but omits photonic band-edge memory and pole residues. The oracle integrates the continuum density and adds discrete poles. The independent reference inverts the full analytic resolvent on a damped Fourier contour without separating atoms in the spectral measure. Direct finite-lambda hopping dynamics and an independently refined inverse contour test the result.
