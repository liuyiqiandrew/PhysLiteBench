# Author analysis

This original anharmonic spectral experiment uses canonical energy fluctuations and normalization from the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams). Its microscopic energies are sums of positive excitations rather than Gaussian extensive variables.

Exactly tilting the Gamma law by exp(−c X) changes its scale to gain/(1+tilt), so the tilted mean is N a_j. The specified entropy gives the exact density identity `exp(N v_j I) p_0(N a_j+sqrt(N) x) sqrt(N)=exp(c sqrt(N) x) p_c^scaled(x)` before integer flooring. Here the right-hand tilted displacement law tends to a Gaussian of variance gain² v_j/(1+tilt)². The high-temperature mean follows by tilting with the actual warm inverse temperature instead. Bounded-order exponential moments of the shifted Gamma law control any linear tilt and the nonnegative quadratic penalty further reduces tails.

The ordinary critical cloud is supported by x≥0 and x+y≥0. On compact sets negative prefix displacements have vanishing expected counts. Boxes strictly in the feasible region concentrate by the exponentially large number of their branching prefixes. First moments bound the Gaussian mass of boundary strips. Multiplying by the finite Gibbs factor gives a tilted two-dimensional Gaussian with a quadratic contrast penalty; the thermal gate is an integral over the marked part of this ordinary cloud divided by its full mass B.

A selected exact site with t=X+Y−N(a_0+a_1) finite has x+y tending to zero and scaled intensity phi_x(x) phi_y(−x) exp(ct) dx dt on x≥0. A strictly positive x gives an atypically low daughter with conditional probability exponentially small at each of exponentially many eligible families. Other daughters of the selected family contribute vanishing relative mass there. The forced site adds W=exp(−ct+c*field*2x−c*curvature*(2x)²) to the ordinary denominator. The pair probability is (W/(B+W))² at that site. The finite-window expectation is the integral of this conditional response against the scaled intensity. The first-moment strip at x=0 is negligible after shrinking the strip.

The oracle completes the two-dimensional Gaussian square and conditions on the sum. The reference integrates the original family coordinate first and uses independent quadrature for the finite rare-site displacement. It checks convergence of the microscopic tilted Gamma law. The shortcut takes both ordinary coordinate displacements to be separately nonnegative, keeping the true rare-site branch calculation.
