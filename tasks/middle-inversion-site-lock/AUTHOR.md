# Author analysis

This original two-setting experiment is inspired by common quenched variables and Gibbs normalization in the [Princeton physics preliminary exams](https://phy.princeton.edu/academics/graduate-program/degree-requirements/preliminary-exams).

For either signed setting, the ordinary critical mass is a Gaussian tilt over the three nonnegative cumulative prefix sums. When a root with finite displacement t is forced, the other descendants of that root give its factor exp(−ct) times a two-level critical mass on its own prefix cone. The ordinary cone concentration follows by counts and shared-prefix covariance on strict positive margins, with vanishing negative-prefix count and shrinking strips controlled by Gaussian moments. These arguments apply to both sign settings on the common sample.

For a chosen root and candidate site with displayed displacements d0,d1, the middle Gaussian is fixed at X_1=(d0−d1)/2 and the last edge is fixed at X_2=−Na(v_1+v_2)+(d0+d1)/2−t. Their joint density in d0,d1 has a factor 1/2 from the Jacobian. The middle Gaussian is evaluated at its center. The last Gaussian cost removes the descendant entropy and yields exp(−N K). After multiplying by N^(3/2) exp(N K), the full count density is proportional to exp(ct+k*((d0+d1)/2−t)) where k=c*(v_1+v_2)/v_2. The finite root window resolves the rare first edge explicitly.

Conditioning on the candidate leaves every other root and every remaining edge at its original distribution. Ordinary descendants of the forced middle edge have vanishing expected normalized Gibbs mass since its energy is near zero rather than the extensive critical edge. Each selected site, however, adds finite mass exp(−c d_j) at both settings. There are consequently three pieces in a denominator: ordinary roots, the forced root's diffuse cloud, and the forced shared site. A marked third draw uses the appropriate piece for its relation to the first edge. All addressed windows are compact and the conditioned event probabilities are bounded.

The oracle integrates the ordinary prefixes in order and changes variables to tagged Gibbs masses for the two line windows. The reference conditions on the last prefix first and quadratures physical line displacements. The completed shortcut omits the selected first edge's diffuse descendants from both denominators, keeping the selected atom and explicit numerator restrictions.
