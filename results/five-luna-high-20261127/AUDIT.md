# Audit of the five Luna High tasks

**All five frozen tasks reproduce their 0/3 results.** Each of the 15 submissions finished, returned finite predictions, passed calibration and failed at least one check of a physical observable. Regrading all 15 submissions, five reference solutions and five completed approximations in the original runtimes reproduced every archived reward and numerical verifier metric exactly. The five reference solutions passed. This audit evaluated the revisions delivered at `743f2d9b`. [Trial manifest](trials.json)

**The clearest evidence of difficulty comes from the slab and one stellar submission.** All three slab submissions changed the film predictions. One stellar submission implemented self-gravity, passed four groups and missed the later echo. For the other eleven submissions, the predictions at their fitted parameter matched the public starter byte-for-byte on every hidden input. Those are genuine failures to predict the requested physics. They establish less about how often a solver that attempts a new physical derivation will succeed.

## Corrections and replay

The pore grader now uses the public calibration residual and intensity range. The model at intensity 0.87312 passes all five tests. Both spin calibration checks now use the same reduced chi-squared. They both reject the audit example at visibility 0.8131. Four additional noncommuting spin preparations cover 10, 11, 19 and 32 sites. The physical solution's RMSE on these cases is 0.0000234 against the 0.008 limit.

The surfactant reference now evaluates the zero-rate-ratio case directly. Its seven-L example gives 0.001810524488, and all 80 original labels reproduce at their published precision. The slab description defines the signed normal field. The stellar diagnostic handles a primary or intercept measurement with `k=[1,2]` under strict floating-point checks. The stellar and surfactant graders also enforce their real-array contracts. Pore, spin, stellar and surfactant now each include a public worked example that distinguishes the starter approximation from the requested physics.

The revised graders passed all five reference solutions. All five completed approximations and all 15 archived Luna submissions passed calibration and failed physical predictions. All five standalone validators passed. These results come from replaying the saved code in the original runtimes with the revised public files and private tests. The fresh Luna batches used the earlier descriptions. The evaluation worktree stores the replay logs and metrics under `jobs/audit-fix-replay-erw3sell/`.

## Scope of the 0/3 result

- **Inputs and provenance.** Within each task, public and private calibration files were byte-identical. The 415 hidden input records were distinct within their tasks and shared no exact input with calibration. Each frozen runtime's `/app` contents matched the checked-in public files, including the slab helpers. The submitted Python files, final event streams and task trees matched the recorded hashes. [Trial manifest](trials.json)
- **Timing.** All 15 event streams ended with `turn.completed` and a final agent message. Harbor recorded a verifier reward and no exception for each. One surfactant run spanned 1,947 wall-clock seconds including 1,121 seconds of recorded host sleep. Its awake solve time was 826 seconds against a 1,800-second limit. The longest verifier took 4.1 seconds. [Run history](README.md)
- **Starter dependence.** At the individual fitted values, all 1,245 hidden outputs were compared with those of the matching public starter. Three pore, three spin, two stellar and three surfactant submissions matched exactly on every input. The three slab submissions and the remaining stellar submission all changed predictions. A public example exercising the distinguishing physics would give solvers a way to discover the limits of the starter before submitting.
- **Selection.** The history contains 21 three-attempt batches across task revisions, including earlier passes and timeouts. The five reported panels are the selected revisions. This audit replayed their saved submissions. An estimate of performance on a new set of attempts would require a separate batch on revisions fixed in advance. [Full revision history](README.md#revision-history)

The five checked-in task validators also passed. In the evaluation worktree, `jobs/legitimacy-audit/` holds the independent calculations under `disorder/`, `slab-spin/` and `stellar/`, the fixed-parameter starter comparisons under `slab-spin/equivalence/`, and all 25 new verifier outputs under `replays/`. The tested replacement for the surfactant calculation is in `disorder/stable.py`.
